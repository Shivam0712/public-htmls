Card 1 of 6010Securitymethodology

What does the master points list specify about each monitored signal, and why must a named party own it before systems integration can succeed?

The master points list is the register of every signal the monitoring systems must receive. For each point, it records its address, scaling (how a raw reading becomes a usable value) and alarm setpoint. A named owner must maintain it as equipment and requirements change; otherwise, the integrator can encounter missing or mis-scaled points late, causing disputed scope, change orders and delayed testing.

Card 5 of 6010Securityresult

What alarm-flood threshold, normal alarm-rate target and priority mix does the report cite, and how can a single power disturbance bury its own root-cause alarm?

An alarm flood is more than 10 alarms within any 10-minute window at one operator position. A healthy system averages fewer than six alarms per operator per hour, with a target mix of about 80% low, 15% medium and 5% high priority. One power dip can trigger hundreds of downstream alarms, overwhelming the operator and obscuring the alarm that identifies the original fault.

Card 8 of 6004Buildingmechanism

When is a structural-steel frame with insulated metal panels attractive, and what span, thermal-envelope, steel-price and severe-wind trade-offs accompany it?

A structural-steel frame clad with insulated metal panels suits multistorey colocation buildings and land-constrained sites. Off-site fabrication enables fast bolted erection, while spans of 12 m or more give flexible layouts. Unlike concrete, steel provides little thermal mass, so the envelope must compensate; costs also fluctuate with steel prices. Severe-wind or tornado exposure requires heavier deck design and careful roof-uplift protection.

How does a roughly 4 ms thyristor-based static transfer switch (STS) differ from an electromechanical automatic transfer switch (ATS), where can it sit, and why is it used for single-corded equipment?

An STS uses electronic thyristors, rather than an ATS’s moving electromechanical contacts, to switch a load between independent sources in about 4 ms; an ATS is slower but simpler. An STS can sit upstream of or within a floor PDU, or near the rack. A rack STS/ATS gives single-corded equipment access to two sources and can keep it powered while one source is shut down for planned maintenance.

Why do the locally adopted NFPA 855 edition and AHJ interpretation of fire-test results affect lithium approval time, and how does the 2026 edition change the approach?

The authority having jurisdiction (AHJ) applies the locally adopted NFPA 855 edition, not necessarily the newest one, so installation requirements vary by location. What document controls, prerequisites, assigned roles, step-level checks, and rollback instructions make a critical-system MOP executable and auditable?

A critical-system Method of Procedure (MOP) identifies its owner, version, dates, objective and scope. It names who authorizes, executes, independently verifies and monitors the work, and lists approvals, equipment checks, tools and safety prerequisites. A passing check permits the next approved step; a failed check stops work, triggers rollback to the last known-good state and escalation.

How do computer-room fire separation, occupancy and egress rules, and local generator or fuel restrictions constrain the placement of halls and support rooms?

Hall and support-room locations must be planned around code boundaries, not just equipment layouts.
