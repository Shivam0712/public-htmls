How do IT load, total facility load, and protected critical load differ, and why is capacity usually expressed in MW of IT power rather than square feet?

IT load is power used by servers, storage, and networking equipment. Total facility load also includes cooling, electrical losses, and other building uses: at a PUE of 1.4, 40 MW of IT load requires about 56 MW total. How is PUE calculated and interpreted, and what do Uptime Institute's 2024–25 averages versus Google's 2025 fleet figure show about non-IT energy overhead?

Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) is total facility power divided by IT-equipment power. A theoretical PUE of 1.0 means no power goes to cooling or other overhead; 1.5 means overhead equals 50% of IT power. Uptime Institute’s 2024–25 survey averages of 1.54–1.56 imply 54–56% overhead, while Google’s 2025 fleet figure of 1.09 implies just 9%.

Card 7 of 87concept

What do N, N+1, and 2N mean for power or cooling resilience, and why does additional redundancy increase construction cost?

N provides exactly the power or cooling capacity required, with no spare. N+1 adds one spare unit, so a component can fail or be serviced while the load remains supported. 2N provides a fully duplicated, independent system without a shared single point of failure. Greater resilience costs more because it requires additional equipment; 2N roughly duplicates electrical gear.
