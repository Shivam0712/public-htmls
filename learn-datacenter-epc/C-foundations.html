How do IT load, total facility load, and protected critical load differ, and why is capacity usually expressed in MW of IT power rather than square feet?

IT load is the power used by servers, storage, and networking equipment. Total facility load also includes cooling, power losses, and other building uses: at a total-to-IT-power ratio of 1.4, a 40 MW IT facility draws about 56 MW overall. Critical load is the portion backed by redundant power, typically IT plus critical cooling. Capacity is quoted in IT MW because power, not floor area, is the scarce resource.

How is PUE calculated and interpreted, and what do Uptime Institute's 2024–25 averages versus Google's 2025 fleet figure show about non-IT energy overhead?

Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) is total facility power divided by IT-equipment power. Card 6 of 87concept

How do white space and gray space differ, what mechanical and electrical systems occupy the latter, and why can their rough planning ratio change?

White space is the floor where IT racks and servers sit. Gray space houses the MEP (mechanical, electrical and plumbing) infrastructure that supports them, including UPS backup power, switchgear, generators and cooling plant. A rough planning estimate gives the two areas about equal space, but greater redundancy or rack density can increase the support area needed.

Card 7 of 87concept

What do N, N+1, and 2N mean for power or cooling resilience, and why does additional redundancy increase construction cost?

N means exactly enough power or cooling capacity to carry the load, with no spare. N+1 adds one spare unit, so a component can fail or be serviced while service continues. 2N provides a fully duplicated, independent system with no shared single point of failure. More redundancy costs more because it requires additional equipment; 2N roughly duplicates electrical gear.

Card 12 of 87concept

What are edge and micro data centers, and how do their size and proximity to users or data sources address latency?

Edge data centers are relatively small facilities, roughly 1–10 MW, placed close to users or the sources producing data. Micro data centers can be as small as a single rack. Card 35 of 87mechanism

Why must site power, redundancy, rack density, cooling approach, and the main electrical design be chosen early rather than changed after construction begins?

Card 72 of 87methodology

What belongs in a 10–20-year facility total-cost-of-ownership model, and how do refresh cycles, depreciation assumptions, and discounting at the owner's cost of capital enter it?

A facility total-cost-of-ownership model combines upfront construction cost, recurring operating costs, and major power-and-cooling equipment refresh over 10–20 years. It distinguishes facility costs from IT equipment, which is typically replaced every 3–5 years and may belong to tenants. Owners commonly assume 15–30 years for facility depreciation or amortization and discount future spending using their cost of capital, often around 8–10%, to compare upfront and lifetime costs.
