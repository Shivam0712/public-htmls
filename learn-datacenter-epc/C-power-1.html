Card 9 of 69mechanism

What three degrees of independence can “two utility feeds” provide, and which types of outage can each arrangement withstand?

“Two feeds” can mean very different levels of protection. Two feeders from one substation bus can withstand a single feeder fault, but not loss of that substation. Feeders from separate substations can withstand one substation’s loss. Feeds using different transmission paths or grid injection points can also protect against a transmission contingency; this is the strongest, rarest, and most expensive arrangement.

Card 10 of 69interpretation

Why does Uptime Institute base Tier reliability on the engine-generator plant rather than the number of utility feeds, and why might dual feeds still have value?

Uptime Institute treats utility power as unreliable for Tier purposes: the on-site engine-generator plant must sustain the data center when the grid fails. Adding utility feeds therefore does not improve its Tier rating. Dual feeds can still improve availability and reduce generator use, but their value depends on independence: two feeders from one substation offer less protection than feeds from separate substations or transmission paths.

How do oil-filled, ester-filled, and dry-type transformers compare on cooling, efficiency, fire safeguards, maintenance, and indoor or outdoor placement?

Oil-filled transformers offer better cooling, efficiency, and power density, so large substation units are normally outdoors; indoor use requires fire separation and liquid containment. Ester-filled units use less-flammable fluid as a middle option. Dry-type transformers avoid flammable liquid and containment sumps, making them standard indoors for UPS-feeding unit substations. Their maintenance relies more on periodic visual and thermal inspections than the more frequent oil testing.

Card 17 of 69mechanism

What does transformer percent impedance control, and why do lower impedance and better voltage regulation come with higher prospective fault current?

Transformer percent impedance (%Z) affects both voltage regulation and the current a transformer can deliver into a short circuit. Lower %Z means less voltage drop as load changes, but it also allows more prospective fault current, requiring breakers with higher interrupting ratings. Higher %Z limits fault current but worsens voltage regulation. For a given transformer rating, fault capacity is approximately kVA divided by %Z expressed as a decimal.

For a 2,500-kVA, 480Y/277-V transformer near 6% impedance, how are full-load current, secondary fault current, and breaker interrupting rating calculated, including impedance tolerance?

For this three-phase transformer, full-load secondary current is 2,500,000 ÷ (1.732 × 480) ≈ 3,007 A, where 1.732 is √3, the three-phase factor for line-to-line voltage. At 6% impedance, the estimated bolted secondary fault current is 3,007 ÷ 0.06 ≈ 50 kA. Allowing the −7.5% impedance tolerance gives a minimum of 5.55% and a higher fault estimate: 3,007 ÷ 0.0555 ≈ 54.2 kA. The report’s indicative breaker selection is ≥65 kA AIC (interrupting capacity).

Card 19 of 69mechanism

Why do transformer core losses continue whenever a unit is energized while copper losses change roughly with the square of loading?

A transformer has two distinct losses. Core, or no-load, loss continues whenever the transformer is energized, even if it supplies no load. Copper loss depends on the current flowing to the load and rises roughly with current squared. Consequently, at 50% of full-load current, copper loss is about 25% of its full-load value, while core loss remains.

When is air-insulated rather than gas-insulated MV switchgear preferable, considering footprint, cost, maintenance, and site conditions?

Air-insulated switchgear (AIS) is the usual indoor medium-voltage choice for data centers when there is room for its larger lineup: it costs less and is easier to maintain. Card 30 of 69mechanism

What is selective coordination during an electrical fault, and how do vacuum breakers, protective relays, device ratings, and engineering studies confine the outage?

Selective coordination means a fault trips only the nearest upstream breaker, leaving other circuits energized. Medium-voltage vacuum breakers act on protective-relay signals, such as overcurrent or differential protection. Engineers study relay trip curves and available fault current, check that breakers can safely interrupt it, and assess arc-flash hazards. Proper settings and ratings isolate the smallest practical zone.

Standby diesel starts quickly, accepts large sudden loads, and uses on-site fuel, but has the highest NOx and particulate emissions; the report cites roughly $1,000/kW as a generator cost benchmark. How does a prime or continuous-duty generator rating differ from an emergency-standby rating, and why does that distinction matter for bridge power?

A prime or continuous-duty rating describes a generator intended to supply power in ongoing operation. An emergency-standby rating applies to a generator used only when normal power fails. Card 43 of 69methodology

Why are routine monthly generator exercises and annual load-bank tests used, and what operational problems are they intended to prevent or detect?

Monthly no-load or light-load exercises keep standby generators in a routine test cycle. How does repeating hall-scale power blocks while expanding the shared substation let the campus grow toward full build-out without replacing Phase 1 equipment?

The shared 138-kV substation and two 30-MVA transformers start with Phase 1. Each later ~10-MW IT hall adds its own unit substations, generators, and UPS blocks to the shared 34.5-kV bus, leaving Phase 1 gear in place. Card 67 of 69interpretation

Why is the explorer’s common 2.5-MW N+1 rule a reasonable UPS planning shortcut but incomplete for generators, and what different load bases and ratings must their formulas use?

A 2.5-MW block is a reasonable UPS planning shortcut because UPSs typically use 2–3 MW blocks and protect critical IT load. Generators must also supply mechanical and other loads, and their ratings are typically around 2.75 MW. Thus, estimate UPS blocks as ceil(IT MW ÷ 2.5) + 1, but generators as ceil((IT MW × PUE) ÷ 2.75) + 1. These are planning heuristics, not verified equipment counts.

What surviving-capacity checks are needed before treating the report’s rounded Phase 1 genset, unit-substation, and LPT counts as N+1 at their stated loads and power factors?

Card 69 of 69open question

What remains unresolved about whether on-site gas plants built in response to interconnection delays are temporary bridge power or a permanent data-center architecture?

Long grid-interconnection queues are prompting data centers to build on-site natural-gas plants for primary power rather than relying only on standby diesel. The report cites a 2.3 GW gas-power order as evidence of the scale of this shift. What remains unknown is whether operators will retire or reduce these plants once grid connections arrive, or keep gas generation as a lasting part of their power architecture.
