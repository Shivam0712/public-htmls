From three tiers to Clos, and what non-blocking costs

Why the industry moved. The three-tier design (access, aggregation, core) was built for north-south traffic and leaned on Spanning Tree Protocol to break loops, which blocked redundant links and left half the fabric idle. Modern workloads, above all AI training, are overwhelmingly east-west, and in a tree two servers in different racks climb to the aggregation or core and back, adding hops, latency and drops. The spine-leaf or folded-Clos topology (Charles Clos, 1953) flattens this: every leaf connects to every spine, any server reaches any other in two hops, ECMP balances across every uplink and no link is blocked. Scaling is linear: leaves for ports, spines for bandwidth (research synthesis).

Oversubscription. The ratio of server-facing to spine-facing bandwidth on a leaf. Legacy enterprise fabrics ran 3:1 or worse; AI back-end fabrics run 1:1, non-blocking, because collectives such as all-reduce synchronize thousands of GPUs at once and stall on the slowest link. On a radix-64 leaf a 1:1 split gives 32 server ports and 32 uplinks; 3:1 gives 48 and 16. Landing the same servers non-blocking therefore needs 50% more leaves, 50% more spines and roughly double the inter-switch optics and fiber. That doubled optics count is the single biggest reason AI fabrics are expensive (§5, §6).

Tiers by scale. A two-tier Clos on radix-64 800G silicon reaches roughly 2,048 GPUs at 1:1 (IP Infusion) (verify: secondary source). Beyond that a super-spine tier interconnects pods and scales a three-stage Clos to 16,000+ GPUs; NVIDIA's reference architecture adds it at roughly 1,024 nodes and above. Rule of thumb: pod = two tiers; hall or campus = three. Each tier adds a hop and a full layer of optics. Hall 1's AI pod, at 50 racks and 900 compute trays, stays under the threshold (§12).

51.2T silicon and tier count. Broadcom's Tomahawk 5 (BCM78900) delivers 51.2 Tb/s on one chip as 64 × 800G, 128 × 400G or 256 × 200G; Marvell's Teralynx 10 is also 51.2T with a radix up to 512 × 100G. One 51.2T switch replaces twelve 12.8T switches (650 Group), and a higher-radix chip can collapse three tiers to two, saving over 1 MW in a large cluster (Marvell). The 2026 successor is 102.4T with 1.6T ports (§5).

Where the switch sits, and the control plane

ToR versus EoR/MoR. Top-of-rack puts a leaf in every rack: server links are short in-rack DAC and only uplinks leave, which minimises long runs but multiplies switches and strands ports in light racks; the failure domain is one rack. End-of-row or middle-of-row consolidates one larger switch or pair per row with every server cabled to the row end, cutting switches and stranded ports but demanding long, dense runs down the row and a bigger failure domain. For AI back-ends copper reach forces a hybrid: leaves sit in an adjacent rack because 800G passive copper reaches about 2 m (§5). The reference facility runs ToR in the 15 and 40 kW pods (the research's fiber arithmetic assumes it), leaves MoR open for the 8 kW retail pod, and puts the AI pod's leaves in a network rack every two GPU racks (§3, §12).

Underlay. RFC 7938 (August 2016) codified eBGP as the single routing protocol for large Clos fabrics: a unique private ASN per ToR (64512–65534), one ASN for all Tier-1 devices, single-hop sessions on point-to-point links, Layer 3 to the ToR. It minimises blast radius; the 2021 Meta outage (§8) is the cautionary tale of BGP withdrawals gone wrong.

Overlay. EVPN/VXLAN tunnels over the Layer-3 underlay give multi-tenancy, Layer-2 adjacency where applications still need it, and workload mobility. Single-tenant AI back-ends often skip it and run pure routed RoCE because every microsecond of encapsulation matters; colo operators offering multi-tenant network services lean on it. In the reference facility the operator's facility LAN and retail services run EVPN/VXLAN; the AI pod's back-end does not.

The four networks of an AI pod, and what a rail is

Four networks (NVIDIA NVL72 AI Factory reference architecture). (1) Back-end GPU compute: east-west RDMA, highest bandwidth, rail-optimized, non-blocking, ConnectX-8 SuperNICs at 800G per GPU operated as 2 × 400G dual-plane. (2) Front-end: north-south node management, storage access and user traffic, each node with a BlueField-3 DPU at 2 × 400G to two switches. (3) Storage, often merged with the front-end at small scale. (4) Out-of-band management, 1 GbE RJ45 to every node's BMC (§8).

Scale-up versus scale-out. Scale-up is NVLink inside the rack, where 72 GPUs form one domain at 900 GB/s per GPU (1,800 GB/s bidirectional) on copper, Topic 8's subject. Scale-out is the network between racks and pods. A collective uses scale-up first, then hands the remainder to scale-out. The boundary is the rack.

Rail. A rail is a same-index NIC position across all GPU servers: GPU 1's NIC on every server connects to the same leaf, GPU 2's to another, and so on. Because dominant all-reduce traffic is same-rail, it stays on one leaf hop; GPUs on different rails or racks traverse the spine. NVL72-class racks use a dual-plane design: each GPU's 800G port breaks into 2 × 400G, one to each of two identical planes, each plane scaling to 1,024 × 400G interfaces.

Scalable unit (verify). The research defines the SU as one GB300 NVL72 rack (72 GPUs, 18 trays, 4 ConnectX-8 SuperNICs per tray) and then quotes reference designs at "8 leaf + 4 spine per 2-rack SU." The two do not agree on the unit; the course keeps the ratio as indicative and counts per two racks (§12).

Floor-plan consequence. Same-rail NICs must reach their leaf within copper distance, so leaves sit in or beside the GPU racks and cable lengths follow the rail map. In the reference row that is GPU, GPU, network rack, repeated: passive DAC from the adjacent rack, AEC from the one beyond, both on the same rail leaf, and only the leaf uplinks leave on fiber (§5).

InfiniBand, RoCEv2 and Ultra Ethernet

InfiniBand (NVIDIA Quantum). NDR 400G per port, XDR 800G (Quantum-X800; the Q3400 switch is 144 × 800G). Lossless by design through credit-based flow control. SHARP performs in-network reduction, summing gradients in the switch and cutting all-reduce traffic up to 9×, InfiniBand's key differentiator. Latency about 1–2 µs. NVIDIA-specific tooling with a Subnet Manager; the highest lock-in.

Ethernet with RoCEv2 (NVIDIA Spectrum-X, Broadcom Tomahawk 5 and 6). RDMA encapsulated in UDP/IP over standard Ethernet; needs PFC plus ECN/DCQCN tuning to stay lossless. Spectrum-X (SN5600 with BlueField-3 or ConnectX-8) claims about 95% effective bandwidth through adaptive routing and SuperNIC packet reordering. Latency about 2–5 µs. The ecosystem choice, and the reference pod's proposal.

Ultra Ethernet 1.0. Released 11 June 2025: an open, multi-vendor, 562-page stack with a new Ultra Ethernet Transport (UET) offering modern RDMA, packet-level multipathing that replaces RoCEv2's head-of-line blocking, and modern congestion control. Backed by AMD, Arista, Broadcom, Cisco, Intel, Meta, Microsoft, Oracle and later NVIDIA; products late 2025 into 2026. The reference pod specifies UEC-ready NICs so the transport can move without re-cabling.

The crossover (Dell'Oro). "When we first initiated our coverage of AI Back-end Networks in late 2023, the market was dominated by InfiniBand, holding over 80 percent share" (Sameh Boujelbene, 15 July 2025). By late 2025 Ethernet "accounted for more than two-thirds of data center switch sales in AI back-end networks both during the quarter and across the first three quarters of the year, up from less than half in the same period last year" (5 December 2025), and for full-year 2025 Ethernet "more than doubles size of InfiniBand" (10 March 2026) (verify: 2026-dated). 2025 is the crossover year, driven by hyperscalers and neoclouds. Broadcom's Hock Tan said AI networking had reached about 40% of AI semiconductor revenue, "almost a situation where stars are aligned," and guided toward 30% as XPUs ramp (Q1 FY2026 call via SDxCentral) (verify: 2026-dated).

Speeds, optics and the physics of reach

Shipping now. 400G and 800G in volume; 800G ports passed 20 million within three years of first shipments, against six to seven years for 400G. 2026 brings 1.6T ports (8 × 200G) and 102.4T silicon. IEEE 802.3dj (200G per lane; 200/400/800G and 1.6T over copper and single-mode to 40 km) is at draft D2.4, tracking to late 2026 with a September 2026 target (verify).

Form factors and power. OSFP (larger thermal envelope, up to about 24 W, favoured for 800G and 1.6T) versus QSFP-DD (denser, tighter thermals). Standard 800G gray optics draw 14–18 W; the DSP alone is 6–7 W, 12–14 W at 1.6T. Linear-drive pluggable optics (LPO) drop the DSP; co-packaged optics (CPO) move the optical engine onto the switch package for the lowest power per port at the cost of field replaceability, with volume ramp expected from 2026 on both InfiniBand and Ethernet.

Optics as share and failure source. Transceivers consume about 50% of a traditional switch system's power and more than 50% of its cost (Broadcom); in a fully loaded 32-port 800G switch the optics alone can exceed 500 W, more than the ASIC. Each optic is an active, laser-bearing component, so thousands of them are the dominant hardware failure source in an AI fabric; hence the operator's spares room and serviceable, air-cooled network racks.

Copper reach at 800G. Each lane runs 112 Gb/s PAM4, about 28 GHz Nyquist; copper loss roughly doubles against 400G and passive reach collapses to about 2 m. IEEE 802.3ck specifies 2 m maximum at 112G. That opens the 3–7 m dead zone, which retimed active electrical cable (AEC, 10–11 W per end) fills.

How much of an AI pod is network

For a GB300 NVL72 cluster (SemiAnalysis, 2025): networking is 15% of total cluster cost for a three-layer InfiniBand network, 18% for four layers; optical transceivers are about 60% of networking cost and 45% of networking power (three-layer, NVIDIA LinkX optics); the back-end is about 85% of networking cost and 86% of networking power; there are about 6 optical transceivers per GPU in rail-optimized designs, and across a million GPUs transceiver power alone approaches 180 MW.

Caveats. (verify) The 6-optics-per-GPU figure is a three-layer InfiniBand number; a copper GPU-to-leaf pod of Hall 1's size with no super-spine lands lower, so the course keeps 6 as the upper bound. (gap) No network weight per rack; no weight breakdown planned. (gap) Cost per rack and per port not quantified (§11).

Reading the breakdown. The chapter's figure shows the research's 200-rack, 24 MW zone: about 1.4 MW in back-end optics (86,400 × ~16 W), about 1.1–1.6 MW in switch electronics and the rest, the remainder in GPU racks and storage, so network is roughly 2.5–3 MW or 10–12% of the zone, consistent with SemiAnalysis's shares. Hall 1's 50-rack pod is a quarter of every line (§12).

The edge of the building

Carrier entry. TIA-942 calls for two diverse entrance facilities fed by separate duct banks and maintenance holes so a single backhoe cannot cut both; its redundancy guidance asks for the two access-provider entrance pathways to be at least 20 m (66 ft) apart. A 40 MW campus should expect four or more carriers, mixing dark fiber (the customer lights it) and lit or wavelength services. Topic 3 scored the Phoenix-leaning site partly for its metro fiber density and two separate routes; the course fixes two entrance rooms on opposite walls of Hall 1, at least 20 m apart, on separate duct banks from those routes.

Meet-me rooms. The neutral room where carriers and tenants interconnect, carrier-neutral (operator-owned) or carrier-owned; a four-hall campus should expect two, one per entrance facility. The course proposes one carrier-neutral, operator-owned MMR at Hall 1's NOC end (Topic 4), cross-connected to both entrance rooms, and a second with Hall 2. The commercial model is a course decision: cross-connects as a recurring operator line, with no count in the research and therefore no revenue figure.

Cross-connects. Physically a single-mode fiber jumper between two customers' patch panels (Equinix standardised to single-mode only on 31 March 2024). Pricing about $100–300 a month (Vendr, 2026) (verify: 2026 pricing), with metro quotes of $220–350. Interconnection is 19% of Equinix's recurring revenue: per its Q4 and FY2024 results (12 February 2025), interconnection revenues "stepped up 9% year-over-year… accounting for 19% of Equinix's recurring revenue," with "more than 482,000 total interconnections." "Interconnection and other" is about 8% of Digital Realty's total revenue (FY2024). Wholesale halls carry far fewer cross-connects because one tenant owns the hall; the reference facility's 40 and 120 kW pods will hand the operator little of this line.

Data center interconnect. Dark fiber (you own the wavelengths) versus lit services; DWDM multiplexing many wavelengths onto one pair; and coherent pluggables, 400ZR and 800ZR (OIF; 800ZR finalised October 2024), which plug into router ports and reach about 80–120 km amplified, with ZR+ to 600–1,000+ km. Latency is about 5 µs per km, the governing number for multi-site training links.

Transit, peering and on-ramps. Transit is paying an upstream for full routes; peering is settlement-free or paid direct exchange, usually at an internet exchange point; cloud on-ramps (Direct Connect, ExpressRoute, Cloud Interconnect) are private paths to hyperscalers. In Phoenix the campus would peer at PHX-IX (verify) and the carrier hotels; in Dallas at the Infomart and the major IXPs.

Delivery and risk. Carrier laterals to a new building can take months and are a pre-opening critical-path item; who pays (carrier, developer or shared) is negotiated. Carrier agreements are a pre-opening milestone and the MMR is an owner deliverable commissioned before the first tenant can light a circuit. On Topic 3's clock the course places carrier agreements at T+6–18 and MMR commissioning at T+36–40 (§11).

Out of band

Architecture. A physically separate management LAN with its own switches and its own uplink, a cellular modem or a second carrier, that must survive a failure of the production fabric. It connects console servers and every device's BMC/IPMI/Redfish port. NVIDIA's reference architecture uses SN2201-class switches (48 × 1G plus 4 × 100G) to reach every node's BMC and every switch's management port, uplinked into a separate core. The reference facility puts one at each HDA and the MDA, on its own power, uplinked through a second carrier at ER-B plus cellular, never through production.

The Meta case. On 4 October 2021 a backbone-audit command withdrew Meta's BGP routes, partitioning its data centers and taking its DNS offline for about six hours. In Meta's words (Meta Engineering, "More details about the October 4 outage," 5 October 2021): "Our primary and out-of-band network access was down, so we sent engineers onsite to the data centers to have them debug the issue and restart the systems." Engineers reached routers' console ports physically because the OOB path shared fate with production. The lesson: the OOB must be independent in path, power and reachability.

What rides it (names only). Facility OT: BMS, EPMS, DCIM, security systems, rack PDU and CDU controllers. Tenant IT: server BMCs, switch management ports. These belong to Topic 10 and later; here they are ports on the OOB LAN, on logically separate segments, often on the same physical plant with VLAN isolation.

The NOC's demands and ownership. Monitoring collectors and remote hands need 1–2 management ports per device on Cat6A at 1–10G (RJ45). The operator owns the facility OOB, the tenant its device OOB inside the cage. The OOB is commissioned first because power, cooling and the fabric cannot be safely brought up without management visibility; the course places OOB and NOC live at T+34–36.

Media, connectors and the standard's spaces

Copper. Cat6: 1G, and 10G only to about 55 m (legacy). Cat6A: 10GBASE-T to 100 m, the workhorse for management and PoE. Cat8: 25/40G to 30 m for short in-row runs. Copper still wins for OOB (Cat6A) and in-rack DAC at 800G (2 m or less); it is dead for high-speed reach beyond a rack.

Fiber. Single-mode OS2 (yellow) has effectively unlimited data-center reach and is the default for new builds. Multimode OM3/OM4/OM5 bought cheaper optics historically, but reach collapses at high speed and it has no 800G+ future beyond SR8. The reference facility specifies OS2 for every fiber run and no multimode, a course decision that trades cheaper optics today for a plant that outlives three optics generations.

Connectors. LC duplex for FR and LR; MPO-12, MPO-16 and MPO-24 for parallel optics; and very-small-form-factor connectors (MMC, SN, CS) at about three times the density: 216 SN-MT or MMC-16 fit where 80 MPO-16 fit. 800G SR8 needs MPO-16 (MPO-12 cannot carry 8 channels); DR8 and 2×DR4 use MPO-12 APC. Base-8 trunks match 8-fiber parallel optics without waste; base-12 is legacy. Pre-terminated, factory-tested trunks beat field splicing in new builds. The open bet is MPO-16 (proven, bulky) against VSFF (dense, immature tooling); guessing wrong means re-patching in three years. The course takes MPO-12 APC on base-8 and defers VSFF.

TIA-942 spaces. ER (entrance room): carrier hand-off and demarcation, two diverse per campus. MDA (main distribution area): backbone hub, core routers, main cross-connect, at least one per hall. IDA (intermediate, TIA-942-B and C): aggregation, zero or one per hall. HDA (horizontal): row or zone distribution, one per pod or several rows. ZDA (zone): optional passive consolidation point, at most 288 connections. EDA (equipment): the rack itself. Hall 1 uses two ERs, one MMR, one MDA, four HDAs plus a reserved fifth, 250 EDAs, no IDA or ZDA.

TIA-942-C (2024) and BICSI 002. TIA-942-C recognises single-pair Ethernet for horizontal cabling, requires at least two Category 6A cables to high-throughput wireless access points, requires at least two fibers for horizontal and backbone, and allows any TIA-568.3-compliant fiber connector outside equipment outlets. BICSI 002 is the companion design and planning standard for spaces, bonding, environment and cable management.

Pathways, testing and management

Pathways. Overhead cable tray, ladder rack and fiber raceway are the new-build default over underfloor. TIA-569 and NEC Articles 725, 770 and 800 govern separation from power to prevent EMI. In the AI pods the ceiling is shared with 120 kW busway and liquid manifolds: busway highest, then cable tray, with manifolds routed so they cannot drip on either; trays never directly under manifold joints; everything seismically braced with access clearance. Fill at or below 40–50%, and multiple trays per row separating fiber, copper and power. The course fixes, with Topics 4, 6 and 7: busway A and B highest, fiber raceway and copper tray on separate tiers below, manifolds lowest and offset over the rack rear so no tray sits under a joint; at least two trays per row plus power.

Bundles as a pathway load. An 800G DAC bundle is 10–14 mm thick; at 64 cables per switch the bundles alone can block airflow and fill a tray. One more reason thin fiber dominates beyond the rack and trays are sized at half fill.

Cross-connect versus interconnect. A cross-connect joins two panels by a jumper through a distributor (structured, flexible); an interconnect goes equipment to equipment (fewer connection points). Structured cabling with panels and trunks beats point-to-point for manageability at scale.

Testing. TIA-568.3 sets insertion-loss limits. Tier 1 measures insertion loss and length with a light source and power meter; Tier 2 adds an OTDR trace. AI-pod fiber typically requires Tier 2 plus manufacturer certification for the 25-year warranty. The reference facility runs Tier 1 on horizontal runs and Tier 2 on every backbone and all AI-pod fiber.

Labeling, bonding and airflow. TIA-606-D governs labeling of every cable, panel and port; TIA-607 governs bonding and grounding. Blanking panels, brush grommets and sealed, firestopped containment penetrations keep hot and cold air apart where cables cross the boundary.

Division 27 and the demarcation

Division 27. Under design-build, structured cabling is Division 27 (communications) low-voltage work, a subcontract under the electrical contractor or a separate low-voltage contractor. Design and installation typically require an RCDD (Registered Communications Distribution Designer) stamp; outside-plant work may need an OSP designation. Materials are owner- or contractor-furnished per the contract. Timing: conduit and tray with the MEP rough-in; trunks and terminations once rooms are weather-tight; certification near the end, before commissioning.

The demarcation. Owner's: entrance rooms and MMR to the MDA, the facility backbone. Negotiable: MDA to a tenant's HDA. Tenant's: everything inside the cage. Wholesale tenants take raw conduit, tray and power and build their own; retail tenants get structured cabling to the cabinet. The owner pre-provisions conduit, tray and fiber trunks to each pod regardless, Topic 2's 15–20% reserve principle applied to cabling, so a tenant can light a pod without construction. In Hall 1 the 8 and 15 kW pods are retail and the 40 and 120 kW pods wholesale.

The AI pod: who pulls and certifies. The cluster vendor, a low-voltage contractor or the tenant may pull and certify the fiber. NVIDIA-path builds often use cluster-vendor-certified teams; otherwise a Division 27 contractor certifies to the connectivity manufacturer's specification to earn the 25-year warranty. Cost per rack and per port rises sharply against general compute, which is why network is 15–18% of AI cluster cost (§6) and a small fraction of a general hall; (gap) the research does not quantify it. Hand-offs to Topic 11, names only: test reports, as-builts, the labeling audit.

What the contract must close. Who certifies the AI pod's fiber, to which tier and labeling scheme; who owns, powers and cools the network racks in the AI rows (on nobody's line item in Topics 2, 4, 6, 7 or 8 yet); and who carries the 800G optics and switch lead time, the 2025–26 pacing item.

What is changing in 2026. Whether open Ultra Ethernet displaces Spectrum-X and the remaining InfiniBand base. 1.6T ports and 102.4T silicon, with 802.3dj completing late 2026.
