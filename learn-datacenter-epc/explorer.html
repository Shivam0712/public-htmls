How these numbers are estimated

Indicative rules of thumb, not a design. Topics 2, 5, 7 and 8 refine every one of them; the model is the estimate() function in site.js.

  • IT load: kW per rack times 1,000 racks. At the fence: IT load times PUE, where PUE is 1.4 to 1.7 for room-level air, 1.25 to 1.45 with containment, 1.2 to 1.35 with rear doors, 1.1 to 1.2 with direct liquid. Regime PUE depends more on the heat-rejection strategy and the climate than on density: a warm-water loop on dry coolers beats a chilled air hall in the same building (Topic 7).
  • UPS blocks: IT load divided by 2.5 MW, rounded up, plus one (N+1). Generators: load at the fence (IT times the mid-range PUE) divided by 2.75 MW, rounded up, plus one, because the engines carry the mechanical plant that the UPS does not (Topic 5: 5 UPS blocks but 6 gensets for 10 MW of IT). Blocks of 2 to 3 MW are the common module; a real design also chooses the redundancy topology.
  • Grid connection: under about 15 MW at the fence, a distribution feeder (12 to 35 kV); 15 to 50 MW, a dedicated customer substation at 69 to 138 kV; 50 to 150 MW, a transmission-fed substation at 138 to 230 kV; above that, a transmission interconnect at 230 to 345 kV, where the queue is measured in years. Boundaries vary by utility, and the interconnection study, not the table, decides (Topic 5).
  • Cooling mode: room-level air is comfortable to about 20 kW a rack; air with containment stretches to 40 to 50; rear-door heat exchangers and hybrids carry the contested middle to about 80 (some vendors claim 100); beyond that, direct-to-chip liquid, with 20 to 30% of the heat still leaving through air. Immersion is an alternative above 100. The thresholds drawn (20, 45, 80) follow the sourced scale in Topic 2, Design Requirements.
  • Computer room: 2.8 m² per rack with air (aisles included), 3.2 with rear doors, 4.0 with direct liquid (CDUs, manifolds, wider aisles). Plant and support: 0.4 to 0.6 m² per kW of IT; the weakest rule here, it falls at campus scale.
  • Per rack: 400 kg plus 8 kg per kW, give or take 20%, below 80 kW; above it, no less than 1,400 kg, the vendor-published 1,400 to 1,600 kg for rack-scale AI (a GB200 NVL72 is about 1,360 kg dry and about 1,500 kg with coolant). Storage racks are sized by drive count, not kW, so the rule does not serve them (Topic 8). Above 1,000 to 1,200 kg most raised floors are at their rating.
  • Build cost: from $8M plus $0.03M per kW-per-rack, to $12M plus $0.05M per kW-per-rack, per MW of IT, excluding the servers. Conventional halls land at $8 to 14M per MW; AI-dense halls higher. For a GPU rack the servers cost several times the building's share.
  • Steps: the slider stops only where a categorical output changes (cooling method, grid tier, what fits, floor) plus the reference hall's four zone densities (8, 15, 40 and 120 kW); between steps the numbers scale linearly and are not shown. scripts/explorer_steps.py derives the list from the model.
  • AI racks: the marked band is 100 to 145 kW, where shipping rack-scale systems sit (NVIDIA GB200 NVL72 at 120 to 132 kW, GB300 NVL72 at 132 to 142 kW). GB300 peaks near 155 kW; Vera Rubin is announced at 120 to 190 kW from H2 2026 and Kyber near 600 kW on 800 VDC in 2027.
