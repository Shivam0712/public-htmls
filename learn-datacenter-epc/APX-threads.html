The arithmetic behind the Topic 12 chapter. Topic 12 has no research dump and adds no new facts: every number below comes from one of the eleven chapters or their appendices, cited as L3-<slug> or APX-<slug> §n. Each thread is worked with the multiplication shown, so a reader who disagrees with a step can see which chapter set it. §7 registers the fifteen places where two chapters gave different numbers for the same thing, with the number the course uses and why; nothing is changed at source here, and rows worth fixing become issues for the review days. What the losses cost. A 2,500 kVA dry-type unit loses roughly $9,400 a year at Topic 5's $0.10/kWh; the UPS loses ten times more per MW, the price of a load that never sees the grid (APX-power-1 §5, §9). At Topic 1's $0.07/kWh (§7(c)) Phase 1's 0.76 MW of loss costs 0.76 × 8,760 × $70 ≈ $0.47M a year.

Three PUEs. The 2.89 MW of mechanical is a residual implied by 1.4, not a plant sum. Topic 7 sums the plant for the same hall: 0.7 MW of losses, 0.3 MW of warm-loop fans and pumps, 1.5 MW of chilled-loop compressors, pumps and fan walls, 12.5 MW at the fence, PUE ≈ 1.25 (APX-cooling §12). Topic 10 adds that at Phase 1 part load the meters read 1.55–1.65 (APX-security-monitoring §12). The chapter draws 1.4 because every rating in Topics 5 and 6 was sized to it; §7(f) keeps all three.

Inside the server. Topic 6 ends at 415/240 V at the rack PDU or a three-phase feed to a 48 V shelf (APX-power-2 §12). A GB300 NVL72 rack's eight ~33 kW shelves feed a 50–54 V busbar at ~94.5% at full load (APX-compute §7). Topic 6's 85.5% PSU figure compares legacy supplies at 120, 208 and 240 V; the ladder uses the shelf (§7(k)).

The pod's twist. In the 200-rack zone 10–12% of 24 MW never reaches a GPU: ~1.4 MW is 86,400 optics at ~16 W, about 6% of the zone, and 1.1–1.6 MW is switch electronics (APX-network §6, §12). Hall 1's pod is a quarter of every line. For a watt bound for the pod: 100 W at the fence, ~71 to IT, ~7 of those to switches and lasers, ~64 to GPU racks and storage, ~60 past the power shelf.

Time. About 5 µs per km, so the carrier hotel is ~50 µs away one way; inside Hall 1 the switches cost more than the glass, 2–5 µs a hop on RoCEv2 (APX-network §7).

Three contracts. The carrier's lateral, ordered at T+6–18 or the MMR opens dark; the owner's Division 27 plant from the ERs and MMR to the MDA and trunks to every pod; the tenant's in-cage fabric, a third clock the owner does not run (APX-network §11). Three testers. The cluster vendor, a Division 27 contractor or the tenant may certify the AI pod's fiber; the 25-year warranty follows whoever certifies, so the contract names the certifier, test tier and labeling scheme first (APX-network §11). Hall 1's pod: 3,600 GPUs, 7,200 × 400G links, ~130 switches, ~21,600 optics as the upper bound, ~25 network racks (APX-network §12).

Why the network rack stands there. At 800G passive copper reaches ~2 m (IEEE 802.3ck) and retimed AEC 5–7 m at 10–11 W an end (APX-network §5). A rail promises that GPU 1 of every rack lands on one leaf, so the leaf must sit within ~7 m of every GPU it serves: two racks. Hence GPU, GPU, network rack down the row, ~100 air-cooled 20–30 kW racks across the campus, outside the 1,000 count and on the same busway pair (APX-power-2 §12, APX-cooling §10).

Follow a BTU, worked

Per rack. The liquid share of a 120 kW rack is 70–80% hall-wide, ~90 kW; the GB300 pod alone runs near 90% (§7(h)). On Topic 7's TCS of 40 °C supply and 48 °C return, 8 K, the flow for 90 kW is 90 ÷ (4.18 × 8) ≈ 2.7 kg/s ≈ 161 LPM, so the research's 150–180 LPM describes the liquid share, not all 120 kW (APX-cooling §10). Topic 8's ~130 LPM at 10 K carries 2.17 × 4.18 × 10 ≈ 91 kW, the same heat at a wider rise (APX-compute §2); Topic 7 owns the number at ~160 LPM (§7(g)). PG25 at ≤ 25 µS/cm with 25–50 µm filtration is a lease term at the quick-disconnect (APX-cooling §9, APX-compute §12).

The heat climbs 28 degrees from the 20 °C chilled supply to the 48 °C TCS outlet and reaches the roof one degree above the 45 °C peak. A one-degree approach is not enough for a dry coil on the hottest day, which is why the dry coolers are adiabatic: Phoenix wet bulb sits far below dry bulb, and reclaimed water on the pads holds capacity in the monsoon week, where the 0.2 L/kWh budget is spent (APX-cooling §4, §11). The chilled loop makes 20 °C water from 45 °C air, a 25-degree lift a compressor pays for.

Per CDU, per main. Six pods of eight racks: each pod's liquid share is 8 × 90 ≈ 0.72 MW, at 8 K ≈ 21.5 kg/s ≈ 1,300 LPM, so one 1 MW-class CDU per pod, seven for six. FWS main 4,500 ÷ (4.18 × 10) ≈ 108 kg/s ≈ 6,500 LPM, 10-inch at 2 m/s. Chilled main 6,200 ÷ (4.18 × 10) ≈ 148 kg/s ≈ 8,900 LPM, 12-inch (APX-cooling §7, §12).

Phase 1's heat by loop (APX-cooling §12): pods' cold plates, 75% of 6.0 MW, ~4.5 MW on the warm loop, no compressor; air zones at 8 and 15 kW and rear doors at 40 kW, ~4.0 MW on chilled water; pod residual air, 25% of 6.0 MW, ~1.2–1.8 MW, 1.5 taken, chilled; in-hall electrical losses ~0.7 MW, chilled, mostly the gallery. Total ~10.7 MW, ~3,040 tons; chilled ~6.2 MW, 58%. Topic 7's chapter breakdown showed the chilled IT as 5.5 MW without the 0.7 MW of losses; its appendix and quiz carry 6.2, which the course uses for the plant and the tank (§7(m)).

Two PUEs by path. Warm side: 0.3 MW of fans and pumps on 4.5 MW, 1.05–1.10. Chilled side: 1.5 MW plus ~0.4 of the 0.7 MW of losses, on 5.5 MW of IT, 1.3–1.4. Blend 12.5 MW for 10, PUE ≈ 1.25; 1.35–1.45 in the monsoon week (APX-cooling §12). The chilled loop carries 58% of the heat and 1.5 of 1.8 MW of cooling electricity, ~80%, which is why the campus at 40 MW, with more load on the warm loop, reads better than Hall 1.

The utility failure, second by second

The chapter's timeline is the record; the systems behind its stages, and the chapter that fixed each (APX-power-1 §12, APX-cooling §7, APX-security-monitoring §9, APX-commissioning §8, APX-network §8): MV relays open on both 138 kV lines and the EPMS sequence of events stamps every breaker on the one clock the BMS shares (Topics 5, 10); six VFI blocks carry 10 MW on LFP battery with A and B drawn from different blocks (5, 6); chillers and dry-cooler fans stop with the mechanical bus while CDU pumps and fan walls are assumed to ride the UPS (verify, APX-cooling §7) and the tank starts carrying the chilled loop (7); six sets crank at 1–3 s, reach rated voltage and frequency by 10 s, NFPA 110 Type 10, and take mechanical load and rectifiers at 10–15 s by closed transition (5); magnetic-bearing chillers restart in about 3 min against the tank's 10 (7); the NOC runs the EOP, confirms redundancy and notifies tenants over the OOB at 15–30 min (11, 9); fuel is resupplied at 24 h against 48 h of autonomy (5); the RCA closes at the change advisory board and the tenant report shows no credit because the bus held and temperatures stayed in band (11).

The alarm flood. A utility loss fans into hundreds of alarms. ISA-18.2 calls more than 10 in 10 minutes at one position a flood; a rationalised system averages under 6 an operator-hour. The one NTP or PTP clock lets the EPMS sequence of events and the BMS log order the fault the same way (APX-security-monitoring §9).

The battery budget. Five minutes held per block; ~15 s spent (20 ms detection, 3 s crank, 10 s rated, 15 s transfer); four and three-quarter minutes of margin for a failed start and retries, of which 45 s is the bound the tank is sized against (APX-power-1 §12, §7(n)).

The tank. 6.2 MW × 600 s = 3,720 MJ; at a usable 10 K and 4.18 kJ/kg·K, 3,720,000 ÷ 41.8 ≈ 89,000 kg, about 90 m³ (APX-cooling §7). It covers the 10–45 s gap plus the 3 min restart with six minutes to spare.

An EOP skeleton for loss of utility, modelled on MOP-EL-014 (APX-commissioning §8); a course example.

  • EOP-EL-001, loss of utility, Hall 1. Trigger: EPMS loss-of-source on both lines, or generator start signal. Roles: shift engineer commands; two technicians walk the yard and UPS rooms; NOC monitors and communicates; OEM field engineers on the 15–30 min response.
  • Decision first: is the critical bus stable? If any block is on bypass or under 20% charge, go to the loss-of-critical-bus EOP.
  • Steps with expected results: six blocks on battery, then on generator [rectifiers back within 15 s]; six sets online and sharing load [34.5 kV bus in tolerance]; chilled pumps, fan walls and CDU pumps running [flow, differential pressure]; chillers restarted [supply under 22 °C within 5 min]; dry coolers and FWS pumps running [36 ± 2 °C]; declare and notify tenants over the OOB within 15 min; read fuel, call resupply if the forecast exceeds 12 h; suspend MOPs and hot work; on return, verify stability and witness the retransfer.
  • Rollback: any failed check stops and escalates; safe state is the generator bus. Completion: log, redundancy confirmed, RCA opened, tenant-report line drafted.

The tenant-report line. "Loss of utility, 02:14, 47 min on generator. Critical bus held; hall temperatures in band; no SLA credit. RCA: utility-side fault, no facility action. Fuel restored to 48 h by 06:00." The monthly report also carries PUE and WUE against the OPR and the maintenance and generator-test logs (APX-commissioning §8).

What fails first

The chapter's compare with the arithmetic behind each row (APX-power-1 §6, APX-power-2 §6, APX-cooling §7).

  • Second 138 kV line. Nothing changes at t = 0: both lines are lost together and the engines are the source regardless; the Tier Standard credits no utility arrangement (APX-power-1 §2).
  • Battery. The critical load drops at t = 0 and returns at closed transition 10–15 s later. Every rack reboots and the pod's training job restarts from its last checkpoint (L3-compute); a typical SLA credit is 1/30th of the monthly charge per breach (APX-commissioning §10).
  • The N+1 genset. 5 × 2.75 = 13.75 MW against 14 MW nameplate; one failed start is 0.25 MW short. Topic 5 lets the diversified design load cover it; the design response is to shed mechanical load first, since the tank is carrying the chilled loop anyway (APX-power-1 §8).
  • 3M2. Every row draws from two of three blocks and none runs above 67%, so a block fault mid-event moves its rows onto a block sized for them. Under plain N+1 on a common bus the reserve is a module, not a path, and a fault can drop rows (APX-power-2 §6, §12).
  • The tank. Without it the chilled loop has no cooling for the 10–45 s before pumps return and the ~3 min until chillers restart. Order of magnitude: ~4 MW into ~12,000 m³ of hall air (14,400 kg) raises it ~0.28 K a second once containment recirculates, so 20 °C passes 27 °C in well under a minute (indicative).
  • UPS on the CDU pumps (verify). The research does not say whether CDU pumps and fan walls ride the UPS; the course assumes yes (APX-cooling §7). If not, cold plates lose flow for the 15 s to generator and this becomes the first failure, since a 1,000–1,400 W GPU with stopped coolant has a time constant of seconds; settle it in the BOD before the CDU order releases.
  • OOB network. If it shares path, power or carrier with production the NOC is blind when it most needs to see. Meta, 4 October 2021: "our primary and out-of-band network access was down, so we sent engineers onsite" (APX-network §8).
  • A drilled EOP. ~40% of operators had a major human-error outage in three years; 85% of those were procedural (APX-commissioning §8).
  • Fuel contract. 48 h is four times Uptime's 12 h minimum, but a multi-day grid event exhausts it; the 24 h contract turns 48 hours into a week (APX-power-1 §8).
