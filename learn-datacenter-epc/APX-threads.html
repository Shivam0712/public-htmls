The arithmetic behind the Topic 12 chapter. Topic 12 has no research dump and adds no new facts: every number below comes from one of the eleven chapters or their appendices, cited as L3-<slug> or APX-<slug> §n. Each thread is worked with the multiplication shown, so a reader who disagrees with a step can see which chapter set it. §7 registers the fifteen places where two chapters gave different numbers for the same thing, with the number the course uses and why; nothing is changed at source here, and rows worth fixing become issues for the review days. Follow a watt, worked

Phase 1's design-load illustration allocates 10 MW to IT and 14 MW at the fence at PUE 1.4. The roughly 4 MW difference already includes cooling, electrical losses and other building loads. Do not add the losses below to 14 MW again (APX-power-1 §6). At full build, the same design ratio gives 56 MW at the fence for 40 MW of IT. What the losses cost. A 2,500 kVA dry-type unit loses roughly $9,400 a year at Topic 5's $0.10/kWh; the UPS loses ten times more per MW, the price of a load that never reaches IT (APX-power-1 §5, §9). At Topic 1's $0.07/kWh (§7(c)), Phase 1's illustrative 0.76 MW of loss costs 0.76 × 8,760 × $70 ≈ $0.47M a year.

Three PUEs. The 2.89 MW of mechanical load is the residual required by the 1.4 design allocation, not a separately measured plant sum. Topic 7's full-load plant sum for Hall 1 instead uses 0.7 MW of losses, 0.3 MW of warm-loop fans and pumps and 1.5 MW of chilled-loop compressors, pumps and fan walls: 12.5 MW at the fence for 10 MW IT, PUE ≈ 1.25 (APX-cooling §12). Topic 10's Phase 1 part-load case with shared plant online gives 15.5–16.5 MW and PUE 1.55–1.65; it is a modeled metering scenario, not a measured Phoenix year (APX-security-monitoring §12). The chapter retains all three: 1.4 for equipment design ratings, ~1.25 for Topic 7's full-load plant sum and 1.55–1.65 for Topic 10's Phase 1 part-load case (§7(f)).

Inside the server. Topic 6 ends at 415/240 V at the rack PDU or a three-phase feed to a 48 V shelf (APX-power-2 §12). The shipping GB300 NVL72 power shelf, with eight ~33 kW shelves feeding a 50–54 V busbar, is ~94.5% efficient at full load (APX-compute §7). Topic 6's 85.5% PSU figure compares legacy supplies at 120, 208 and 240 V; the ladder uses the shipping shelf (§7(k)). The course's 120 kW rack position is an allowance, not a GB300 rating: a GB300 at 132–142 kW nominal exceeds it (APX-compute §2).

The pod's twist. In the 200-rack AI zone, 10–12% of 24 MW goes to its network rather than GPU computation: ~1.4 MW is 86,400 optics at ~16 W, about 6% of the zone, and 1.1–1.6 MW is switch electronics (APX-network §6, §12). Hall 1's pod is about a quarter of each line. Topic 9 places roughly one network rack per two GPU racks: ~100 air-cooled network racks campus-wide sit outside the 1,000 counted positions, but their ~2–3 MW must be allocated inside the relevant phases' IT allowance, not silently added to the 38.9 MW rack sum or the 40 MW rounded plan (APX-network §12; APX-power-2 §12). For an illustrative pod watt bound: of 100 W at the fence, ~71 goes to IT, ~7 of those watts to switches and lasers, ~64 to GPU racks and storage, and ~60 past the power shelf. That split assumes the supporting network draw has been budgeted within IT.

Their roughly 2–3 MW belongs inside the applicable phases' IT allowances, rather than being added without a change to the 38.9 MW rack plan.

Follow a BTU, worked

Per rack. A 120 kW rack's hall-wide planning liquid share is 70–80%, or ~90 kW; the GB300 pod alone can approach 90% (§7(h)). On Topic 7's technology cooling system (TCS) temperatures of 40 °C supply and 48 °C return, an 8 K rise, flow for 90 kW is 90 ÷ (4.18 × 8) ≈ 2.7 kg/s ≈ 161 L/min. Thus the research's 150–180 L/min describes the liquid share, not all 120 kW (APX-cooling §10). Topic 8's ~130 L/min at 10 K carries 2.17 × 4.18 × 10 ≈ 91 kW, the same heat at a wider rise (APX-compute §2); Topic 7 supplies the ~160 L/min course operating point (§7(g)). PG25 at ≤ 25 µS/cm with 25–50 µm filtration is a lease term at the quick-disconnect (APX-cooling §9, APX-compute §12).

The heat climbs 28 degrees from the 20 °C chilled supply to the 48 °C TCS outlet. A dry coil in 45 °C air cannot cool 46 °C return water to 36 °C. The roof coolers have adiabatic spray, but it can meet that target only within verified wet-bulb performance and reclaimed-water supply and WUE limits; otherwise sized refrigeration must carry that hour (APX-cooling §4, §11). W40 describes the allowed inlet temperature for qualified IT equipment, not an FWS pipe rating. The chilled loop makes 20 °C water from hot outdoor air, a lift paid for by a compressor.

Per CDU, per main. Six pods of eight racks group 48 of the roughly 50 first-phase AI racks, with the remaining roughly two positions to be included in the final CDU grouping and capacity check; seven 1 MW-class CDUs are planned as six duty and one spare (APX-cooling §7, §12). Each eight-rack group's liquid share is 8 × 90 ≈ 0.72 MW, at 8 K ≈ 21.5 kg/s ≈ 1,300 L/min. FWS main: 4,500 ÷ (4.18 × 10) ≈ 108 kg/s ≈ 6,500 L/min, 10-inch at 2 m/s. Chilled main: 6,200 ÷ (4.18 × 10) ≈ 148 kg/s ≈ 8,900 L/min, 12-inch (APX-cooling §7, §12).

Phase 1's heat by loop (APX-cooling §12): pod cold plates, 75% of 6.0 MW, send ~4.5 MW to the 36/46 °C warm loop; 8 and 15 kW air zones and 40 kW rear doors send ~4.0 MW to chilled water; pod residual air, 25% of 6.0 MW, contributes ~1.2–1.8 MW, with 1.5 MW taken; and ~0.7 MW of in-hall electrical losses also reaches the chilled side, mostly in the gallery. Total heat removal is ~10.7 MW, ~3,040 tons; chilled duty is ~6.2 MW, 58%. Topic 7's chapter breakdown showed 5.5 MW of chilled IT without the 0.7 MW of losses; its appendix and quiz carry 6.2 MW, the figure used for the plant and tank (§7(m)).

Two PUEs by path. Warm side: 0.3 MW of fans and pumps on 4.5 MW, 1.05–1.10. Chilled side: 1.5 MW plus ~0.4 of the 0.7 MW of losses, on 5.5 MW of IT, 1.3–1.4. Topic 7's illustrative full-load plant sum gives 12.5 MW for 10 MW IT, PUE ≈ 1.25, with a 1.35–1.45 monsoon-week case (APX-cooling §12). The chilled loop carries 58% of the heat and 1.5 of 1.8 MW of cooling electricity, ~80%; these cases are not annual measurements.

The ten-minute tank condition. At 6.2 MW, ten minutes of usable chilled storage is 6.2 × 600 = 3,720 MJ. The calculated reserve helps only while appropriately powered pumps keep water circulating through the tank and coils (APX-cooling §7).

The utility failure, second by second

This is an illustrated test sequence, not proof of installed performance (APX-power-1 §12, APX-cooling §7, APX-security-monitoring §9, APX-commissioning §8, APX-network §8). At T+0, utility supply is lost on both 138 kV lines; MV protection opens the dead-source path, and EPMS breaker events use a clock shared with BMS (Topics 5, 10). By T+20 ms, the LFP batteries feed the continuously operating inverters of six VFI blocks protecting 10 MW of IT, with A and B rack routes drawn from different blocks (Topics 5, 6). The 20 ms marker is not an output interruption. Chillers and dry-cooler fans may stop with the mechanical bus; CDU pumps and fan walls riding the UPS remain an assumption (verify, APX-cooling §7). If confirmed, their demand must be added to protected-capacity and battery-runtime sizing. The chilled tank carries load only while its circulation pumps remain powered.

At T+1–3 s, six sets crank. Around T+10 s, the illustrated engines reach rated voltage and frequency; that marker is not proof of NFPA 110 Type 10 compliance. Type 10 separately requires designated emergency loads to receive power at their transfer-switch terminals within ten seconds. At T+10–15 s, the example transfers away from the dead utility source to generator supply, an open-source transfer, not a closed transition; generator pickup and UPS rectifier restart must be verified against the protected load. Closed transition between two live sources is a possible operation only on controlled retransfer after the utility recovers (APX-power-1 §12). Six installed 2.75 MW sets cannot carry the 14 MW Hall 1 design load with one unavailable: five provide 13.75 MW at nameplate before hot-site derating (APX-power-1 §8).

Around T+3 min, the selected magnetic-bearing chillers recover, subject to a witnessed full-load restart. The tank's T+10 min chilled-storage target covers the restart only if pumps stay powered and the required temperature and flow remain in range (APX-cooling §7). The NOC follows the EOP, checks available redundancy and contacts tenants over the OOB route at about T+15–30 min (APX-commissioning §8; APX-network §8). A T+24 h fuel-delivery target is set against 48 h of planned usable on-site fuel at the required load; delivery is not guaranteed, so forecast and arrange resupply before the reserve is exhausted (APX-power-1 §8). The RCA goes to the change advisory board, and the tenant report records actual bus, temperature and SLA results, rather than assuming no credit (APX-commissioning §8).

The alarm flood. A utility loss can produce hundreds of alarms. ISA-18.2 calls more than 10 alarms in 10 minutes at one position a flood; a rationalised system averages under 6 per operator-hour. A shared NTP or PTP time source lets EPMS and BMS records put the fault in the same order (APX-security-monitoring §9).

The battery budget. LFP runtime is five minutes per block at its specified load. The illustrated pickup uses ~15 s: 20 ms for the battery-takeover marker, 1–3 s to crank, near 10 s to rated condition and 10–15 s for pickup. Roughly four and three-quarter minutes remain on that simplified clock, subject to actual loading, battery condition and any protected pumps or fans. The 45 s retry bound is a tank-design case, not proof that an unsuccessful generator start can always be recovered (APX-power-1 §12, §7(n)).

The tank. 6.2 MW × 600 s = 3,720 MJ; at a usable 10 K and 4.18 kJ/kg·K, 3,720,000 ÷ 41.8 ≈ 89,000 kg, about 90 m³ (APX-cooling §7). If circulation and temperature limits are maintained, it covers a 10–45 s interruption case and a roughly 3 min chiller restart with nominal time remaining. Stored energy alone does not prove rack cooling.

An EOP skeleton for loss of utility, modelled on MOP-EL-014 (APX-commissioning §8); a course example.

  • EOP-EL-001, loss of utility, Hall 1. Trigger: EPMS loss-of-source on both lines, or generator start signal. Roles: shift engineer commands; two technicians check the yard and UPS rooms; NOC monitors and communicates; OEM field engineers are requested on the 15–30 min response.
  • Decision first: is the critical bus stable? If any block is on bypass or under 20% charge, go to the loss-of-critical-bus EOP.
  • Steps with expected results: six blocks on battery, then generator input [rectifiers restored after verified pickup]; sets online and sharing the approved protected load [34.5 kV bus in tolerance]; chilled pumps, fan walls and CDU pumps running [flow and differential pressure, with UPS feed unverified]; chillers restarted [supply under 22 °C within 5 min]; dry coolers and FWS pumps running [36 ± 2 °C when hot-weather mode can deliver it]; declare and notify tenants over OOB within 15 min; check usable fuel and call resupply if the forecast exceeds 12 h; suspend MOPs and hot work; on utility return, verify a stable live source and witness retransfer.
  • Rollback: stop and escalate on any failed check; the intended safe source is the generator bus if it can carry the approved load. Completion: record actual capacity and alarms, open the RCA and draft a tenant-report line.

An illustrative tenant-report line. "Loss of utility, 02:14, 47 min on generator. Critical bus and temperatures: record witnessed traces and any excursions. RCA pending; assess SLA credit under the lease. Fuel inventory and resupply status: record verified amounts and delivery." The monthly report also carries PUE and WUE against the OPR, plus maintenance and generator-test logs (APX-commissioning §8).

What fails first

This is the chapter's one-component-out comparison, with outcomes conditional on the actual distribution path, approved load and witnessed test (APX-power-1 §6, APX-power-2 §6, APX-cooling §7). The order is a way to examine the failure chain, not a prediction that every listed part fails at a fixed time.

  • Second 138 kV line. In the illustrated simultaneous utility loss, losing the second line does not change the immediate source: the engines must carry the load. A different utility fault and actual route diversity need their own test; the Tier Standard credits no utility arrangement (APX-power-1 §2).
  • Battery. Without UPS ride-through, a loss of utility can interrupt the critical bus during the illustrated 10–15 s open-source transfer. Which racks drop depends on their actual feeds and alternate sources, not a presumed closed transition. If a training job loses power, it may restart from its last checkpoint (L3-compute); any SLA credit depends on the lease and recorded breach, rather than an automatic 1/30th-month award (APX-commissioning §10).
  • The generator spare. Five surviving 2.75 MW sets give 5 × 2.75 = 13.75 MW at nameplate against the 14 MW Hall 1 design load, a 0.25 MW shortfall even before hot-site derating. The locked six-set yard is therefore not N+1 for that load (APX-power-1 §8). The owner needs an approved change: a seventh set, larger site-rated sets or a revised protected-load requirement. Do not assume diversity closes the gap. Load shedding helps only if a revised, approved requirement still protects the necessary IT and cooling load.
  • 3M2. Each row has two feeds from separate blocks in the six-block layout, with blocks normally near 67% loading. Whether a block fault leaves a row powered depends on separation, breaker action, capacity on its surviving feed and the witnessed one-out test; losing 3M2 does not necessarily drop rows. Plain N+1 on a common bus supplies a spare module, not an independent path, so a common-path fault can drop rows (APX-power-2 §6, §12).
  • The tank. Without usable storage, the chilled side has less protection during the 10–45 s transfer and roughly 3 min chiller restart. If circulation or fan power stops, stored cold water cannot protect the racks. An indicative bound puts ~4 MW into ~12,000 m³ of hall air, about 14,400 kg, for a potential ~0.28 K/s rise after containment recirculates; the real inlet response depends on airflow, load and thermal mass (APX-cooling §7).
  • UPS on the CDU pumps (verify). The research does not establish whether CDU pumps and fan walls ride UPS; the course assumes they do for its illustrated recovery (APX-cooling §7). If they do not, cold-plate flow may stop until generator pickup and become the earliest thermal problem. A 1,000–1,400 W GPU with stopped coolant has a time constant of seconds. Settle pump and fan feeds in the BOD, add confirmed protected loads to UPS and battery sizing, and witness flow during transfer before releasing the CDU order.
  • OOB network. If repair access shares the failed production path, supply or carrier, the NOC may be blind. Meta, 4 October 2021: "our primary and out-of-band network access was down, so we sent engineers onsite" (APX-network §8).
  • A drilled EOP. Roughly 40% of operators had a major human-error outage in three years; 85% of those incidents involved procedures that were ignored or flawed. Rehearse the actual isolation and recovery path (APX-commissioning §8).
  • Fuel contract. The planned 48 h usable reserve is four times Uptime's 12 h minimum at the required load, but its depletion time varies with load and available fuel. A 24 h resupply target does not guarantee delivery or turn 48 h into a week. Test the contract, inventory and multi-day contingency (APX-power-1 §8).

Capex by system. At the $450M midpoint, Turner & Townsend's air-cooled benchmark shares imply electrical 54% ≈ $243M, mechanical 22% ≈ $99M, shell 14% ≈ $63M and GC 10% ≈ $45M (APX-foundations §9). They are indicative construction shares, not an exact allocation for this mixed-cooling campus. Topic 10's component ranges are physical security $8–20M, fire detection and suppression $10–25M, and monitoring and controls $10–25M: $28–70M together, inside the electrical, mechanical and shell cost lines rather than on top of campus capex. Against a $400–500M campus, the range is about 6–18% (APX-security-monitoring §12; §7(q)).

Phase 1 opex. An illustrative 12.5–14 MW × 8,760 h × $0.07 gives ~$7.7–8.6M a year of electricity, mostly passed through. Maintenance at 3% of ~$180–230M is ~$5.4–6.9M; insurance at 0.7% is ~$1.3–1.6M; payroll for 25–32 people at $110–150k loaded is ~$3–4.5M; tax, water, connectivity and G&A add $2–4M. This gives about $20–25M a year for Hall 1 versus roughly $65M a year at full-campus use, including ~$34M of electricity at $0.07/kWh (APX-foundations §10, APX-commissioning §10). These are load-conditioned illustrations, not an annual Phoenix meter record.

Retainage and damages. On ~$200M of agreed Phase 1 construction price, 5% retainage is ~$10M withheld from payments: half released at T+42 and half at T+54 after the 12-month defects period, not $10M of extra expense. Delay liquidated damages of 0.1–0.5% a day are $0.2–1M a day, capped at 5–10%, or $10–20M; tenant SLA damages can exceed that cap and leave the owner with the gap. OEM warranties on gear delivered at T+30–34 may already have run for months by T+42 (APX-commissioning §7).
