Glossary

One plain-English line per term, added as each chapter is written.

Course & system terms

[R] / [W]
topic research mode — Desktop Research feature vs Claude Code's own web search (see PLAN.md).
Wrap
the close-out procedure (/wrap) that updates state, syncs issues, regenerates docs, and lands a wrap: commit.

Data center terms

CDU
Coolant Distribution Unit — the interface between the building's water loop and the liquid-cooling loops inside a rack.
CxA
Commissioning Agent — the independent third party who verifies systems perform as designed. Independent on purpose: they work for the owner, not the designer or builder.
DBB / DB
Design-Bid-Build (design and construction contracted separately, one after the other) / Design-Build (one firm does both).
DCIM / BMS / EPMS
Data Center Infrastructure Management / Building Management System / Electrical Power Monitoring System — three overlapping monitoring stacks that watch IT, building, and electrical systems.
Direct-to-chip / immersion cooling
bringing liquid to the chip via cold plates, or submerging hardware in non-conductive fluid. Required above roughly 50 kW per rack.
EPC
Engineering, Procurement, Construction — a delivery model where one contractor owns all three, concentrating schedule and performance risk in a single party.
Interconnection
the utility's process of studying and connecting a large new electrical load to the grid. A multi-year process, not a wiring job.
IST
Integrated Systems Test — the "pull the plug" rehearsal that fails everything at once to see whether the facility really rides through.
ITAD
IT Asset Disposition — secure disposal or resale of retired equipment, including data sanitization (NIST 800-88).
Long-lead equipment
gear whose manufacturing time dominates the project schedule — transformers, switchgear, generators. Ordered before the design is finished.
OCP / ORv3 / 48V busbar
Open Compute Project and its Open Rack v3 spec; the shift from 12V to 48V DC rack power to feed high density.
OPR / BOD
Owner's Project Requirements / Basis of Design — what the owner needs, and how the design intends to meet it. Commissioning tests against these two documents.
PUE / WUE / CUE
Power / Water / Carbon Usage Effectiveness — total facility resource use divided by IT resource use. Lower is better; all three are easy to game.
Rack density (kW/rack)
power drawn, and therefore heat produced, per rack. The number that drives power, cooling, and structural design.
Spine-leaf (Clos)
the flat, scalable network topology used inside data centers. EVPN/VXLAN are the overlay protocols run on top of it.
Tier (Uptime) / Rating (TIA-942) / Class (BICSI)
three competing schemes for classifying facility redundancy and availability, I–IV or equivalent. They are not interchangeable.

Reader site terms

Bus / bus section / breaker
in a single-line diagram, the main conductor, a stretch of it, and the switch that separates two stretches.
Figure directive
a :::kind block in chapter markdown whose data the site generator draws (numbers, breakdown, timeline, flow, compare, scale).
Read more
the collapsed block under a figure that holds the depth; a chapter must read with every one of them closed.
Single-line diagram
the electrical drawing that shows a facility as one bus with loads tapped off it; the course map borrows the form.

Topic 1 — Foundations, delivery models, economics

$/MW
construction cost per megawatt of IT load; almost always excludes the IT gear, and scope (shell-only vs all-in, land in or out) varies by source.
AI factory
a dense, single-workload, usually single-tenant campus built for GPU training or inference at 30–130 kW per rack, liquid-cooled.
Build-to-suit
a developer builds an entire single-tenant facility to a hyperscaler's specification, then leases it to them.
Capex
capital expenditure, the one-off cost of building the facility.
CM at risk (CMAR) and GMP
the construction manager joins early to advise on design, then commits to a Guaranteed Maximum Price; overruns above it are the CM's, savings are often shared.
Colocation (retail vs wholesale)
a provider rents space, power and cooling to tenants who bring their own IT. Retail sells per rack with power bundled on 1–3 year terms; wholesale sells whole halls per kW on 5–15 year leases.
Contingency
a cost buffer for the unknown; explicit and shared in a GMP, buried in the price under lump-sum contracts.
Critical load
the part of the load carried on protected, redundant (UPS and generator-backed) power; roughly IT plus critical cooling.
Edge data center
a small facility (roughly 1–10 MW, down to a single rack for "micro") placed near users or data sources to cut latency.
Enterprise data center
a facility owned and run by one organisation for its own IT.
Escalation clause
the contract term that says who pays when materials and equipment prices rise between signing and delivery.
Facility load
IT load plus cooling, electrical losses and ancillary loads; what the utility sees at the fence. IT load times PUE.
FIDIC Silver Book
the international EPC/turnkey contract form from process industries; fixed price, fixed date, maximum risk on the contractor.
Force majeure
the clause excusing delay caused by defined events outside either party's control.
Hyperscale
very large (~100 MW and up) standardised facilities built or leased by cloud and platform operators such as AWS, Microsoft, Google, Meta.
IT load
the power drawn by servers, storage and network alone; the number a data center's size is quoted in.
Liquidated damages
a pre-agreed sum the contractor owes per day of late completion, standing in for the owner's actual losses.
MEP
mechanical, electrical and plumbing; the power and cooling systems that make up 75–80% of a data center's construction cost.
Modular / prefabricated
a construction method, not a facility type: factory-built power, cooling or data-hall modules assembled on site.
N / N+1 / 2N
exactly enough capacity; one spare unit beyond the need; a fully duplicated independent second system. Topic 2 owns the reasoning.
Neocloud
a GPU-first cloud operator (CoreWeave, Nscale) that typically leases powered shells rather than building its own.
Opex
operating expenditure, the recurring annual cost of running the facility; electricity dominates.
Owner's engineer
the owner's technical adviser who reviews the design against the owner's standards.
Owner's representative
the owner's project-management proxy for schedule, budget and stakeholders.
Owner-furnished equipment (OFE)
long-lead gear the owner buys directly and hands to the contractor to install; starts the equipment clock early but moves delivery and integration risk to the owner.
Powered shell
building, land and utility power delivered by the provider; the tenant fits out the electrical and mechanical interior.
Rack unit (U)
1.75 inches of vertical space in a rack; a standard rack holds about 42U.
TCO
total cost of ownership; capex plus every year of opex and refresh over the facility's life, usually discounted to present value.
Turnkey
a contract that hands over a complete, working facility for a fixed price; the usual meaning of EPC in data centers.
White space / gray space
the IT floor where racks live / the support area holding UPS, switchgear, generators and cooling plant.

Topic 2 — Requirements, capacity and availability

AHJ (Authority Having Jurisdiction)
the local building or fire official who decides which code editions apply at a site and enforces them, local amendments included.
AI-ready
a label that means something only when it names numbers: kW per rack, floor loading, supply-water temperature, GPM per rack, CDU space.
ASHRAE 90.4 (MLC / ELC)
the data center energy standard; compliance is two ratios against maxima, the Mechanical Load Component (cooling power over IT power) and the Electrical Loss Component (power-chain losses). PUE is defined but not used for compliance.
ASHRAE Guideline 0 / Standard 202
the commissioning-process guideline and standard that make the OPR the yardstick the finished facility is tested against.
Availability class (EN 50600 / ISO/IEC 22237)
the European framework's Class 1–4, basic to fault tolerant, set separately for power, cooling and cabling, plus Protection Class 1–4 for physical security.
Change order
an amendment to a signed contract that changes scope, price or time; how a vague requirement becomes the owner's bill.
Code / standard / guideline / certification
legal minimum rules enforced by the AHJ / detailed good practice, binding only when a code adopts it by reference / advisory best practice / third-party verification against a standard.
Concurrent maintainability
any component can be taken out for maintenance without shutting down IT, which needs redundant components and redundant paths (Uptime Tier III).
Containment (hot / cold aisle)
walls, doors and roofs that keep cold supply air and hot exhaust from mixing; needed from roughly 10 kW per rack, and air's practical limit sits near 20 kW.
Density profile / pod
the distribution of kW per rack across the zones of a hall, never an average; a pod is a group of cabinets sharing one power and cooling rating, and no cabinet may exceed the pod's peak.
Design load / nameplate / actual load
what the facility is sized to carry after diversity and headroom / the sum of equipment ratings / the real operating draw. Each is smaller than the last; the power block is sized to the first.
EPO (Emergency Power Off)
the single-action disconnect that kills power to an IT room; one of the NEC Article 645.4 conditions.
Fault tolerance
any single failure is survived automatically because two active paths run in physically separated rooms (Uptime Tier IV).
NEC Article 645
the optional National Electrical Code article for IT rooms; an owner opts in by meeting 645.4 (dedicated HVAC, restricted room, fire-rated separation, EPO) in exchange for wiring flexibility.
NFPA 75 / 76 / 855
fire protection of IT equipment / of telecom facilities / of energy-storage systems; the last is reshaping UPS battery rooms as lithium-ion replaces lead-acid.
Performance specification
a specification that states the outcome to be achieved (Tier III, PUE ≤ 1.4, 10 MW) and leaves the means to the contractor; the basis of EPC risk transfer.
Phasing (day-one vs ultimate)
building shell and site for the ultimate load but MEP for the first block only, adding plant as leasing and interconnection arrive.
RDHx (rear-door heat exchanger)
a liquid-cooled coil on the back of a rack that removes exhaust heat before it reaches the room; bridges roughly 20–80 kW per rack.
Reference design (owner standard)
a repeatable, pre-engineered building block an owner reuses across sites under a master agreement; the hyperscaler way to keep cost predictable.
Stranded capacity
capacity paid for but unusable because a different resource (power, cooling, busway, floor) ran out first.
TCDD / TCCF / TCOS
Uptime's Tier Certification of Design Documents / Constructed Facility / Operational Sustainability. Ask for the TCCF for the specific hall; many plaques are design-only.

Topic 3 — Site, grid interconnection, water and procurement

Air permit (major / minor source)
the clean-air authorisation any on-site combustion needs; staying under minor-source thresholds is the fast path, and the permit, not the turbine, is what stalls bridging plants.
Behind-the-meter (BTM) / bridging power
generation on the owner's side of the utility meter; a bridge covers the gap until grid interconnection is complete, and increasingly stays as prime power.
Blowdown
the concentrated, mineral-laden water purged from an evaporative cooling loop; it must go to sewer or to a permitted discharge.
CIAC (contribution in aid of construction)
the non-refundable payment a customer makes toward utility-owned facilities built to serve it.
Cluster study
a utility or RTO study of many interconnection requests at once, allocating shared network upgrades among them; deposits are what make a cluster shrink to its real size.
Conditional-use permit (CUP)
the discretionary local approval a data center usually needs even on industrial land; its public hearing is where projects most often die.
Consumptive vs non-consumptive use
water withdrawn and not returned locally (evaporated) vs water that goes back to the system after use.
Contract demand
the megawatts a large customer reserves under its tariff; minimum-take clauses bill a share of it whether used or not.
FAT (factory acceptance test)
witnessing equipment pass its tests at the factory before it ships; an owner obligation under OFE.
Free-cooling hours
hours a year a site can reject heat without mechanical refrigeration; set by climate, so a site-selection economics factor.
Free-issue
handing owner-bought equipment to a contractor to install; the contract must say what happens if it arrives late or damaged.
GOES (grain-oriented electrical steel)
the core steel of transformers; about 70% is made in Japan, South Korea and Germany, which is part of why transformer lead times doubled.
GSU (generator step-up transformer)
the transformer between a generator and the grid; the longest lead time of any, ~144 weeks in 2025.
Large-load interconnection
the state-level, utility-run process to connect a big new electricity consumer, distinct from FERC's generator interconnection.
Large-load tariff (GS-5, E-67, SB 6)
a rate class for big customers with long terms, minimum take, collateral and exit fees; Dominion's GS-5, SRP's E-67 and Texas's SB 6 are 2025 examples.
Minimum take / take-or-pay
a tariff clause requiring payment for a set share of reserved capacity regardless of use (AEP Ohio 85%, SRP 80%).
Network upgrades
transmission or distribution reinforcements a new load triggers beyond its own tie; identified in the system impact study.
NPDES permit
the Clean Water Act discharge permit that sets temperature and chemistry limits on effluent such as blowdown.
OFE / CFE
owner-furnished vs contractor-furnished equipment; who buys the long-lead gear, holds the factory slot and carries its delay.
Powered land
a parcel valued mainly for secured, near-term access to electricity; what developers now hunt for first.
Prior appropriation / riparian rights
western US water law ("first in time, first in right"; junior holders curtailed first) vs eastern law (reasonable use tied to waterfront land).
Reclaimed water (purple pipe)
treated non-potable water delivered in a separate line; the politically safest data center supply where it exists.
RTM (release to manufacture)
the point a factory actually starts building; it can lag the purchase order by a quarter, and the lead time counts from here.
Slot reservation / framework agreement
a deposit that holds a factory production slot, or a multi-site supply agreement with an OEM; how gear is bought before design is finished.
System impact study (SIS) / facilities study
the utility's model of a new load's effect on the grid and the upgrades it triggers / the engineered and priced design of the substation, tie and metering.
WUE (water usage effectiveness)
litres of water per kWh of IT energy; ~1.8 industry average, 0.15 at best, ≤ 0.2 the reference campus target.

Topic 4 — Building and physical infrastructure

AC156
the shake-table test protocol that certifies a rack or piece of equipment survives seismic forces; the rack maker supplies it, the building supplies the anchorage.
Column grid (bay)
the spacing of structural columns, typically 8.4–12 m in data halls; it sets how many rack rows and aisles fit between columns.
Dry-in (weathertight)
the milestone when roof and walls keep the weather out; nothing electrical or mechanical inside can start before it.
Dunnage
steel support frames that spread the weight of rooftop equipment such as dry coolers and chillers onto the roof structure.
Freeboard
extra height added above a design flood elevation as a safety margin; the finished floor sits above the 500-year level plus freeboard.
Governing envelope
the heaviest and largest piece of equipment the rigging path must ever carry, used to design doors, corridors and slab before the equipment list is final.
IMP (insulated metal panel)
factory-made steel-skinned cladding with an insulating core, the usual envelope on a steel-framed data hall.
k-value (modulus of subgrade reaction)
the stiffness of the ground under a slab; it governs a slab-on-grade's load capacity more than the soil's bearing strength does.
Knock-out panel
a wall section designed to be removed so heavy gear can be rolled in after the shell is closed; provided where the equipment list is not yet known.
Manifold
the pipe header that distributes coolant from a CDU to the racks in a row; hangs lowest in the overhead stack so a leak falls on nothing electrical.
Meet-me room
the carrier-neutral space where tenants and network providers interconnect; allocated as floor area here, its contents belong to Topic 9.
Point load vs distributed load
weight concentrated on a small area (a rack caster, a CDU foot) against weight spread evenly across a floor (psf); a floor can pass the second and fail the first.
Post-tensioned floor
a concrete slab pre-compressed with steel tendons so it can span between columns on upper storeys; the multi-storey substitute for slab-on-grade.
Precast concrete
structure and wall panels cast in a factory and shipped to site; cures in about 48 hours in the plant against 28 days cast in place.
psf / kN/m² / kg/m²
units of floor loading; 1 kN/m² is about 20.9 psf or 102 kg/m². Data halls specify 250–400 psf where the code minimum is 100.
Raised access floor (RAF)
tiles on pedestals over a plenum used for underfloor air and cable; typically rated 1,200–1,500 kg/m² and not built for a 1,500 kg rack.
RFS (ready for service)
the date a hall is energized, commissioned and handed over; T+42 for reference Hall 1.
Rigging path
the route heavy equipment travels from truck to pad; it must carry the heaviest load that ever moves along it, so it often governs the slab more than the racks do.
Risk Category (IBC/ASCE 7) and Ip
the occupancy class that sets design forces; data centers are usually Category IV, which gives the component importance factor Ip = 1.5 and about 50% higher anchorage forces.
SDC (Seismic Design Category)
the A–F class combining a site's shaking (SDS) with its Risk Category; engineered anchorage is required from C upward.
Shell and core
the structure, envelope and base building, the box before MEP fit-out; 10–15% of a data center's construction cost.
Slab-on-grade
a concrete floor poured directly on prepared ground; 12–18 in thick in a data hall against 4–6 in in an office.
SPMT (self-propelled modular transporter)
a multi-axle powered platform used to move the heaviest loads, such as a 100–200 t transformer, from road to pad.
Tilt-up
concrete wall panels cast flat on the slab and craned upright; the fastest cheap shell for a big single-storey box on a flat site.
Truck court
the paved manoeuvring area in front of the dock doors; 120 ft or more deep so trailers and cranes can work.

Topic 5 — Power: utility to UPS

AIS / GIS
air-insulated versus gas-insulated switchgear; GIS is compact and sealed, historically filled with SF6.
Arc-resistant switchgear (IEEE C37.20.7)
gear tested to vent an internal arc away from people; Type 2B keeps them safe with the control doors open.
Belly tank / bulk tank
the sub-base fuel tank under a genset for immediate supply, and the shared storage that provides the autonomy target.
BESS (battery energy storage system)
batteries beyond UPS ride-through, at the MV bus for peak shaving or grid services, at LV for smoothing AI transients.
Block (power block)
the unit of design, one 2.5 MW UPS with its ~2.75 MW genset and 2,500 kVA unit substation; halls grow by repeating it.
Demarcation point
the ownership boundary between utility and customer, usually the incoming structure or first high-side breaker; revenue metering sits there.
Double-conversion (VFI) / line-interactive (VI)
IEC 62040-3 UPS classes; VFI rebuilds the waveform so the load never sees mains, VI passes mains and corrects voltage.
DRUPS
diesel rotary UPS, a flywheel coupled to a diesel; 13–40 s of inertia bridges to the engine, no battery room.
Dry-type vs oil-filled transformer
dry-type is the indoor default for unit substations (no flammable liquid); oil-filled is the outdoor default for LPTs and triggers NEC 450 indoors.
EPA Tier 2 / Tier 4 Final
nonroad engine emission tiers; Tier 2 is allowed for emergency standby only, Tier 4F with SCR and DPF is required once a set runs for demand response or bridging.
Fuel polishing
filtering stored diesel to remove water and microbial growth so it starts an engine years after delivery.
HVO
hydrotreated vegetable oil, a drop-in renewable diesel with up to ~90% lower lifecycle CO2.
Impedance (%Z)
the transformer property that sets fault current (≈ kVA ÷ %Z) and voltage regulation; 5.75% nominal at 2,500 kVA.
Large power transformer (LPT)
the HV-to-MV substation transformer, 30–100 MVA, oil-filled, outdoors; the 128-week item.
LFP / NMC / VRLA
lithium iron phosphate, nickel-manganese-cobalt and valve-regulated lead-acid batteries; LFP is the data center lithium default.
Main-tie-main (double-ended)
two sources and a bus tie, so either side can be maintained with the tie closed and no load dropped; the physical form of concurrent maintainability.
NFPA 110 Type 10
emergency power at the transfer switch within 10 seconds of utility failure; the standby standard for critical facilities.
Paralleling switchgear
gear that synchronizes several gensets onto one bus for flexible N+1; long-lead in its own right.
Ride-through
the seconds a UPS carries the load from stored energy while the generators start; ~15 s used of 5 min held.
Selective coordination
a protection scheme in which the nearest upstream breaker clears a fault, isolating the smallest zone; proven by a study someone must own.
SF6-free switchgear
MV gear insulated by vacuum plus dry air or a fluoronitrile mix instead of SF6 (GWP ~23,500); the 2026 spec, with a 60 Hz caveat.
Static bypass
the UPS path that passes mains straight to the load on an internal fault or overload; eco-mode runs on it by choice.
Unit substation
the packaged MV-to-LV transformer and switchgear (34.5 kV to 480Y/277 V, 2,500 kVA) at the hall wall feeding UPS and mechanical plant.

Topic 6 — Power: distribution and redundancy

3M2 / 4M3 (distributed redundant)
three (or four) independent UPS systems that share the load so any one can be lost; each runs at ≤ 67% (≤ 75%) and every rack row takes A and B from two different systems. The reference hall's Tier III model: six 2.5 MW blocks for 10 MW usable.
415/240 V distribution
three-phase 415 V line-to-line, 240 V line-to-neutral to the rack, so the server takes 240 V with no PDU isolation transformer; a 60 A circuit carries ~34.5 kW against 17.3 kW at 208 V. Raises available fault current.
A/B feed / dual-corded / single-corded
two independent power paths to a rack; dual-corded gear has a PSU on each and needs no switch, each side loaded ≤ 50%; single-corded gear needs a rack ATS or STS to see both.
Arc-flash boundary / incident energy
the distance at which an arc would deliver 1.2 cal/cm² to skin; PPE categories map to incident energy and no energised work is permitted above 40 cal/cm² (NFPA 70E).
ATS (automatic transfer switch)
an electromechanical switch that moves a load between two sources; slower and simpler than an STS, common at the rack for a single-corded appliance.
Autotransformer
a single-winding transformer that shifts voltage a small step (480 → 415 V) without creating a separately derived system; about 90% smaller than a 208 V isolation transformer.
Availability (A = MTBF / (MTBF + MTTR))
the fraction of time a repairable element works; parallel paths multiply their unavailabilities. Industry "nines" are legacy arithmetic, not Uptime certification, which dropped them in 2009.
Block redundant (catcher / reserve bus)
N active UPS blocks plus one unloaded reserve that a failed block's load reaches through an STS; ~80% usable, but a large STS sits in every path.
Busway (busbar trunking)
prefabricated overhead conductor track (40–1,250 A) into which tap-off boxes twist-lock while live; the row distribution layer for Zones 2–4 and 94% of the reference hall's megawatts.
CBN / SRG (common bonding network / signal reference grid)
the equipotential grounding structure of a data hall; a copper grid on 0.6–3 m centres, ≥ #6 AWG, per IEEE 1100 and TIA-942, with every cabinet bonded to it directly.
Chapter 3 wiring (NEC)
the ordinary wiring methods of the National Electrical Code; building an IT room to them instead of Article 645 removes the EPO requirement along with 645's wiring relaxations.
Fault current (available) / AIC / SCCR
the current a short circuit can draw at a point in the system; every breaker (interrupting capacity, AIC) and assembly (short-circuit current rating, SCCR) must exceed it. ~17.6 kA with a PDU isolation transformer, ~56 kA without.
Isolation transformer / separately derived system
a PDU transformer that changes voltage and creates a new neutral and ground reference; the 480 → 208/120 V legacy that 415/240 V distribution removes, along with its impedance.
K-rated transformer (K-13, K-20)
a transformer built to tolerate the extra heating of harmonic currents; K-13 suits modern mixed loads, K-20 is checked where thousands of identical GPU supplies switch in step.
Mt Diablo / ±400 V DC sidecar
the OCP specification (Microsoft, Meta, Google) for a separate power rack that feeds adjacent compute racks at ±400 V DC, 800 V rail to rail; racks from 100 kW to 1 MW; hardware 2026–27. The reference hall reserves bays for it.
MTBF / MTTR
mean time between failures / mean time to repair. Pair MTBF ≈ MTBF² / (2 × MTTR), so repair speed is worth as much as a third path.
NFPA 70E / energised electrical work permit
the US electrical safety standard for working on live gear; data centers work energised, so the permit and arc-flash labels are routine rather than exceptional.
PDU (floor power distribution unit)
a floor-standing cabinet, 300–1,000 kVA, with an input breaker, an optional transformer and branch panels; the first box after the UPS output bus. ~20 in the reference hall's Phase 1.
PFC (active power factor correction)
the front end of a modern server power supply that draws near-sinusoidal current at under 5% THD; why harmonics stopped being a data center problem.
Rack PDU (rPDU)
the 0U vertical outlet strip in a rack, typically 30–60 A at 415/240 V, that turns one branch circuit into server receptacles; per-outlet metering on it proves the A/B mapping.
RPP (remote power panel)
a breaker panel on the floor near the racks, fed by cable home-runs from the PDU; cheaper than busway on day one and the reference hall's choice for Zone 1's stable 8 kW racks.
STS (static transfer switch)
a thyristor switch that moves a load between two sources in ~4 ms; used at the rack for single-corded gear, avoided as a large central unit because it makes one failure into a row outage.
Tap-off box / whip
the twist-lock unit that draws a branch circuit off live busway, and the flexible cable drop from it (or from an RPP breaker) to the rack; ~$350 and owner-installed on busway.
TIA-607-C bonding hierarchy
the standard's primary and secondary bonding busbars and rack bonding busbars, 1/0 AWG to the busbar and ≥ 6 AWG to each cabinet, never bonded in series.
TN-S
an earthing arrangement with separate neutral and protective earth conductors throughout; the preferred scheme for IT loads.

Topic 7 — Cooling and thermal management

Adiabatic dry cooler
a fan-and-coil heat rejecter that runs dry most of the year and sprays water on its air inlet only on hot hours; the reference campus's warm-loop heat sink, fed with reclaimed water on peak days.
Approach temperature
the temperature gap across a heat exchanger, for example between the server loop and the facility loop in a CDU; commercial units achieve about 3–4 °C.
ASHRAE air classes (A1–A4, H1)
server-inlet temperature and humidity envelopes; all A classes recommend 18–27 °C and allow up to 32, 35, 40 or 45 °C; H1 is a tighter class for dense air-cooled gear.
Blanking panel
a plate that fills an empty rack slot so hot exhaust cannot recirculate to the intakes; the cheapest part of containment and the first to be forgotten.
Bypass / recirculation
the two airflow faults containment kills; cold supply short-circuiting to the return without cooling a server, and hot exhaust re-entering a server intake.
CFM per kW
the airflow a rack needs per kilowatt; about 157 at an 11 K (20 °F) rise, 125 at 25 °F, 105 at 30 °F, from ΔT = 3.1 × W ÷ CFM.
CRAC / CRAH
computer-room air conditioner, with its own compressor, and computer-room air handler, a coil and fan on chilled water; the fan wall is the CRAH grown to the size of a wall.
Cube law (fan affinity)
fan power scales with the cube of fan speed, so running at 80% speed takes about half the power; why big slow fans win.
Dew-point control
holding humidity by absolute moisture (about −9 to 15 °C dew point) instead of relative humidity, which means different moisture at each temperature.
EC fan
electronically commutated fan with a brushless DC motor and built-in speed control; the unit of a fan wall and the reason it can be redundant at the fan.
Economizer (air-side, water-side)
using outside air, or outside air via a heat exchanger, to cool without running a compressor; available about 40% of the year in Phoenix, none of it at the 45 °C peak.
ERF (energy reuse factor)
the share of a data center's energy exported as reused heat; Germany's EnEfG mandates 10, 15 and 20% for new centers from 2026, 2027 and 2028.
Fan wall
a floor-to-ceiling array of EC fans and chilled-water coils in a gallery beside the hall, about 800 kW per unit, redundant at the fan; the air backbone of the hyperscale hall since 2024.
FWS (facility water system)
the owner's water loop between the CDU and the heat rejecters; the reference campus runs it warm, W40, at 36 °C supply and about 46 °C return, so it needs no chiller.
In-row cooling
a cooling unit placed between racks, close-coupled to the load; suits spot density in 20–60 kW rows.
Low-GWP refrigerant (A2L / A1)
refrigerants with low global-warming potential mandated by the AIM Act and F-gas rules; A2L (R-1234ze) is mildly flammable, A1 (R-515B) is not, which changes the code path.
Magnetic-bearing chiller
an oil-free centrifugal chiller whose shaft floats on magnetic bearings; excellent at part load and low lift, restarts in about 3 minutes, and runs warm setpoints; the reference plant's chiller type.
PFAS
per- and polyfluoroalkyl "forever chemicals" in the dielectric fluids that two-phase cooling used; 3M's exit and EPA and EU rules pushed the market back to single-phase water-glycol cold plates.
PG25
25% propylene glycol in water, the standard coolant of the server loop; freeze and corrosion protection at a small penalty in heat capacity.
Quick disconnect (QD)
a blind-mate, drip-free coolant coupling at the rack where the server loop meets the rack manifold; the demarcation between Topic 8's rack and Topic 7's heat.
Stratified thermal storage tank
a tall chilled-water tank holding cold water under warm across a thermocline; sized in minutes to carry the chilled loop from generator start to chiller restart, 10 minutes in the reference.
TCS (technology cooling system)
the high-purity server-side loop from the cold plates through the rack manifold to the CDU; about 40 °C out and 48 °C back in the reference pod, ≤ 25 µS/cm and < 50 µm.
Ton of refrigeration
12,000 BTU/h of cooling; 1 kW of IT is 3,412 BTU/h or 0.284 tons, so 10.7 MW is about 3,040 tons.
W-class (W17–W+)
ASHRAE liquid-cooling classes named by their maximum supply-water temperature; W32 is chiller-free in most climates, W40 and above reject on dry coolers year-round.

Topic 8 — AI/HPC, compute and storage

800 VDC
rack power distribution at 800 V DC, arriving with megawatt-class racks in 2027; it moves more than 150% more power through the same copper as 54 V and removes the ~200 kg of busbar a 1 MW rack would otherwise need.
All-reduce
the collective step in which every GPU in a training job exchanges and sums gradients; its alternation with compute is the ~1 Hz power swing the busway sees.
Bare metal
servers run without a hypervisor; the norm for large training because it exposes GPU topology and removes virtualisation overhead.
Bulk-synchronous parallel (BSP)
the compute → barrier → checkpoint cycle of a training job; the reason 200 racks behave as one load with no diversity to bank on.
Checkpoint
a periodic save of a training job's model and optimizer state (140–420 GB for a 70 B model) so the job can restart after a fault; also the stall visible on the busway while it is written.
Cold plate
the liquid-cooled metal block clamped to a GPU or CPU; the point where Topic 8's heat becomes Topic 7's problem.
Compute tray
a slide-in unit of an NVL72 rack holding 4 GPUs and 2 Grace CPUs with cold plates and quick-disconnects; 18 per rack.
DAS / SAN / NAS / object storage
disks inside the server / block storage over a dedicated network / file storage over IP / a scale-out HTTP object store; four tiers, each a different row in the hall.
Data gravity
the pull that keeps a tenant in place because moving petabytes is slow and costly.
EIA-310
the 19-inch rack standard; it fixes mounting width, U-height and hole pattern only, never depth or load rating.
Factory integration / rack-and-stack
a rack shipped fully populated and tested from the vendor / a rack populated server by server on site; AI pods use the first, general zones may still use the second.
GPUDirect Storage
a direct path from storage into GPU memory that bypasses the CPU, 40+ GB/s.
Grace / Vera
NVIDIA's Arm server CPUs paired with GPUs in a superchip; 36 per NVL72 rack.
HBM (high-bandwidth memory)
stacked memory beside the GPU die (HBM3e, HBM4); the main driver of GPU power.
HGX node
an 8-GPU server on NVIDIA's HGX baseboard, ~10–14 kW; three fill the reference 40 kW rack.
Kubernetes / Slurm
the container orchestrator that runs everything that is not training / the HPC batch scheduler that runs the training cluster on bare metal; Slinky combines them.
Kyber / Oberon
NVIDIA rack architectures; Oberon is the current NVL72 frame, Kyber the 2027 ~600 kW class on 800 VDC.
NVL72 / rack-scale system
a factory-integrated, liquid-cooled rack whose 72 GPUs act as one accelerator over NVLink; the reference pod's rack class.
NVLink / NVSwitch
NVIDIA's in-rack scale-up interconnect, 130 TB/s all-to-all on ~3.2 km of copper, no optics.
NVMe-oF / disaggregated storage
flash pooled over a fabric and composed on demand (for example VAST DASE); the storage that sits in flash rows beside the pod.
Parallel file system
storage (Lustre, GPFS, WEKA, VAST) that feeds many GPUs at once at GB/s to TB/s.
Power shelf
the rack-level AC-to-DC conversion unit feeding a 48–54 V busbar; ~33 kW each, eight per NVL72; GB300 shelves carry 65 J per GPU of capacitance.
Power smoothing
rack capacitors and software floors that cut the grid-facing peak of the ~1 Hz swing by ~30%; smoothing, not ride-through, so no UPS relief.
Quick-disconnect (UQD)
the dripless coolant coupling on each tray and at the rack manifold; the boundary between Topic 8 and Topic 7, and where the IT vendor's warranty stops.

Topic 9 — Network and structured cabling

Base-8 trunk / MPO / VSFF
a pre-terminated fiber cable in multiples of eight fibers, matching 8-fiber parallel optics such as DR4; MPO-12/16 are the multi-fiber push-on connectors it ends in; VSFF (SN, MMC, CS) are the very-small-form-factor successors at about three times the density.
BMC / IPMI / Redfish
the baseboard management controller inside every server and switch, and the two interfaces used to reach it over the OOB.
Cat6A / Cat8
twisted-pair copper for 10G to 100 m (also PoE) / 25–40G to 30 m; in the reference facility Cat6A is for OOB, cameras and sensors only.
Co-packaged optics (CPO) / LPO
the optical engine moved onto the switch package / a pluggable with the DSP removed; both attack the fact that optics are about half a switch's power.
Cross-connect / interconnect
a single-mode jumper between two parties' panels in the MMR, sold by the operator at ~$100–300 a month / a direct equipment-to-equipment link inside a cage, free.
DAC / AEC / AOC
passive copper (~2 m at 800G) / retimed active copper (5–7 m) / active optical cable (to ~100 m); the three ways to leave a NIC.
DCI / 400ZR / 800ZR / DWDM
data center interconnect between buildings or sites; coherent pluggable optics reaching 80–120 km from a router port; the multiplexing that puts many wavelengths on one fiber pair.
Division 27 / RCDD
the construction specification division for communications cabling, and the Registered Communications Distribution Designer whose stamp its design and installation usually require.
DR4 / DR8 / FR4 / LR4 / SR8
optics named by reach and fiber: parallel single-mode to 500 m / duplex single-mode to 2 km / to 10 km / multimode to 100 m. The reference plant is single-mode, so SR8 is not used.
eBGP / RFC 7938 / ECMP
external BGP as the fabric's only routing protocol, a private ASN per rack, Layer 3 to the ToR / equal-cost multipath, spreading flows across every spine link.
ER / MDA / HDA / EDA (TIA-942 spaces)
entrance room / main distribution area / horizontal distribution area / equipment distribution area: carrier hand-off, hall hub, pod closet, the rack itself. IDA and ZDA are optional.
InfiniBand / SHARP
NVIDIA's lossless, credit-based fabric, and its in-network reduction that sums gradients in the switch, up to 9× less all-reduce traffic; the tenant's option in the reference pod.
MMR (meet-me room)
the carrier-neutral room where carriers and tenants cross-connect; an owner deliverable commissioned before the first circuit.
OOB (out-of-band network)
the management LAN that shares nothing with production: own switches, own power, own uplink; commissioned first.
OS2 / OM4
single-mode (yellow) and multimode (aqua) fiber grades; OS2 has effectively unlimited data-center reach and is the reference default.
Oversubscription (3:1 / 1:1)
server-facing over spine-facing bandwidth on a leaf; 1:1 is non-blocking and costs 50% more leaves and spines and about twice the optics.
Radix / 51.2T
the number of ports on a switch chip; a 51.2T chip is 64 × 800G and replaces twelve 12.8T switches, which is what keeps a pod at two tiers.
Rail (rail-optimized)
the same-index NIC of every GPU server landing on the same leaf, so all-reduce stays one hop; the copper that carries it cannot be longer than a rack is wide.
RoCEv2
RDMA over Converged Ethernet; needs PFC and ECN tuning to stay lossless. The reference pod's proposed back-end.
Scale-up / scale-out
NVLink inside the rack (Topic 8) / the fabric between racks and pods (this topic); the boundary is the rack door.
Super-spine
the third Clos tier that joins pods; needed above roughly 1,024 nodes and avoided until then because it adds a full layer of optics.
Tier 1 / Tier 2 fiber testing (OTDR)
insertion loss and length with a light source / the same plus an OTDR trace; Tier 2 is what AI-pod fiber and the 25-year warranty require.
ToR / EoR / MoR
a leaf in every rack / one switch at the row end or middle; ToR trades switches for short cables, EoR the reverse.
Transit / peering / IXP
paying an upstream for full routes / exchanging traffic directly, usually settlement-free, at an internet exchange point.
Ultra Ethernet (UEC / UET)
the open, multi-vendor AI Ethernet stack, spec 1.0 released 11 June 2025, with its own transport; the reference pod's NICs are UEC-ready.

Topic 10 — Security, fire, monitoring and efficiency

Alarm rationalisation (ISA-18.2)
the discipline of making every alarm actionable with a cause, consequence, response and priority; a flood is more than 10 alarms in 10 minutes at one operator position.
Anti-passback
an access rule that refuses a credential a second entry until it has recorded a matching exit; defeats badge hand-back.
ASD / VESDA (aspirating smoke detection)
a pipe network that samples air continuously and detects smoke at about 100× lower concentration than spot detectors; EN 54-20 Class A is "before visible smoke".
BACnet / Modbus
the two building-automation protocols; BACnet is what a BMS speaks natively, Modbus is what most power and mechanical gear speaks, and bridging one to the other is routine integration work.
Clean agent (HFC-227ea, FK-5-1-12)
a gaseous fire suppressant that leaves no residue; both named agents are leaving new builds, one under the AIM Act phase-down, the other after 3M's PFAS exit.
Deflagration venting / explosion prevention (NFPA 68 / 69)
a panel that relieves a battery-room gas explosion vs a system that stops one forming; NFPA 855 (2026) requires prevention, not venting alone.
Door-fan (room-integrity) test
a pressurisation test proving a gas-suppression room holds 85% of design concentration for 10 minutes; a schedule gate the AHJ witnesses.
Eco-mode (UPS)
a bypass mode giving 98–99% efficiency at the cost of dropping to IEC 62040-3 Class 3 protection; refused by the reference hall.
EED Article 12 (EU reporting)
the Energy Efficiency Directive rule that every EU data center above 500 kW reports PUE, WUE, ERF and REF annually by 15 May; produced voluntarily here for EU tenants and lenders.
EN 50600-2-5 protection class
the European standard's four-layer physical-security model; at least three classes are required.
FM Global DS 5-32 / 5-33
the insurer's data sheets for data centers and electrical systems, applied on top of code by an FM-insured owner; their recommendations often become deal terms.
IEC 62443 (zones and conduits)
the OT cybersecurity standard that segments control systems into zones joined by controlled conduits; NIST SP 800-82 Rev 3 adopts the same model.
Inert gas (IG-541 / IG-55 / IG-100)
argon, nitrogen and CO₂ blends that hold room oxygen below combustion; clean, PFAS-free and the durable gaseous choice, used here in electrical rooms.
M-rating / K-rating (ASTM F2656)
a vehicle barrier's crash rating, a 15,000 lb truck stopped at 30/40/50 mph (M30/M40/M50), with a P1–P3 penetration suffix; M50/P1 at the reference campus gate.
Mantrap / portal
an interlocked two-door entry that admits one verified person per cycle; about five people a minute by design.
MSI (master systems integrator)
the contractor that ties BMS, EPMS and DCIM together and maintains the points list.
Off-gas detection
hydrogen, CO and HF sensors that catch a lithium cell venting before it ignites; the battery room's first alarm.
OT (operational technology)
the control-system estate (BMS, EPMS, SCADA, DCIM) as distinct from corporate IT, with its own security rules and its own network segment on the OOB.
Points list
the register of every monitored point with its address, scaling and alarm setpoint; the most fought-over integration document, owned here by the owner's controls lead.
pPUE (partial PUE)
PUE for one subsystem or pod, such as a liquid-cooled AI pod at ~1.02–1.1; not comparable across pods.
Pre-action sprinkler
a dry sprinkler system charged only when a detector operates, and in double interlock only when a head also opens; water reaches only the heads that fused.
PUE category (0–3)
where the IT number is measured: an estimate, UPS output, PDU output or the rack-PDU; most quoted PUEs are Category 1 and never say so.
SCADA
supervisory control for the industrial side of the plant, here generator paralleling and switchgear, speaking OPC UA, DNP3 and IEC 61850.
UL 9540A
the thermal-runaway fire-test method whose data drives battery-room spacing, venting and AHJ and insurer approval.
Water mist (NFPA 750)
fine-droplet suppression using 70–90% less water than sprinklers; FM-approved for all data center areas including lithium batteries since January 2023.

Topic 11 — Commissioning, operations and decommissioning

BICSI 009
the open-consensus data-center operations and maintenance standard (2019, updated 2024), the operational complement to BICSI 002; it requires SOPs, MOPs and EOPs.
Black-building test (pull the plug)
the Level 5 climax: with load banks at design load the utility feed is opened on purpose; pass means the critical bus never dropped, the room stayed in band and every step fired in order.
Certificate of destruction
the serialized document an ITAD vendor issues per drive or asset stating how it was sanitized or destroyed; the tenant's proof, only as good as the chain of custody behind it.
Chain of custody
serialized, sealed and witnessed tracking of media from de-install to final disposition; broken the moment a hand-off goes unscanned or a subcontractor goes undisclosed.
Change advisory board (CAB)
the operations body that approves any procedure or change that alters the state of a critical system; every MOP passes it.
Clear / Purge / Destroy
NIST's three sanitization levels: software erase; erase resistant to lab recovery, media reusable; physical destruction.
Cryptographic erase
purging a drive by destroying its encryption key; the reliable method for SSD and NVMe, where overwriting is inadequate.
Defects liability period
the 12 months (typically) after substantial completion in which the contractor fixes defects under warranty and the second half of retainage is held.
EOP (emergency operating procedure)
the drilled, decision-first response to an alarm or fault: safe state first, redundancy restored, fault isolated.
Levels 1–5 (commissioning)
factory witness, receiving, start-up, functional and integrated systems test; an industry convention layered on ASHRAE Guideline 0 and Standard 202, widening from one component to the whole plant.
Load bank
rented equipment that turns electricity into heat so an empty hall can be tested at design load; resistive, reactive, DC or, for AI pods, ~1 MW liquid-cooled units plumbed into the CDU loop.
M&O Stamp of Approval
Uptime's operations assessment, independent of Tier, covering staffing, maintenance and management; two-year validity.
MOP (method of procedure)
a step-by-step script for one action that changes the state of a critical component, with an expected result per step and a rollback; the control on the moment redundancy is deliberately reduced.
NAID AAA / R2v3 / e-Stewards
ITAD vendor credentials: unannounced audits of destruction and security (i-SIGMA) / recycling, custody and downstream vendor management (SERI) / the same plus strict export bans.
NETA ATS-2025 / MTS-2023
acceptance-testing and maintenance-testing standards for electrical power equipment; ATS is run before energization by a NETA Accredited Company.
NFPA 110 Chapter 8
emergency power testing: weekly inspection, a monthly 30-minute run at 30% load, annual load-bank test, four hours every 36 months; on a hot-metro site the air permit caps the hours.
NFPA 70B / 70E
electrical equipment maintenance (a standard, not a recommended practice, since 2023) / electrical safety in the workplace.
NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2
the media-sanitization guideline (26 Sept 2025), which dropped per-media tables in favour of IEEE 2883, separated verification from validation and confirmed one overwrite pass clears an HDD.
Punch list
the priced list of incomplete or defective items outstanding at substantial completion; its value is deducted from the payment.
Remote hands
on-site technicians who perform physical tasks on tenants' equipment for a fee; a billable line in the operations headcount.
Retainage
the 5–10% of each progress payment withheld until completion; commonly half released at substantial completion and half after the defects period.
Service credit (SLA credit)
the tenant's remedy for a breached availability or temperature band, typically 1/30th of the monthly charge per event; paid from the operator's opex.
SOP (standard operating procedure)
the framework for routine, repeatable work, rounds, tickets, BMS operation; its goal is consistency.
Substantial completion / final completion
usable for its purpose, the gate that pays the balance, stops delay damages and starts the warranty / every punch item closed and the last retainage released.
Wet stacking
unburnt fuel fouling a diesel generator run too lightly; the reason NFPA 110 demands a monthly run at 30% load or more.
