Course Index

Layer 1 — Topology · Last updated 2026-08-30

A four-layer self-study course on building a data center from scratch — 14 days, about two hours a day. Each topic has a sixty-word summary, a Layer 3 chapter of readable working knowledge, and an Appendix with the deep detail and sources.

The course runs owner → project → systems → handover: what you are building and what it costs, then where it goes and whether you can get power, then the systems themselves, then proving it works and running it.

0 of 12 topics complete. Greyed links are chapters not yet written.

Topics

  1. 1 Foundations, Delivery Models & Project Economics pending

    What a data center is and the types that exist (enterprise, colocation, hyperscale, edge, modular, "AI factory"); the facility lifecycle from plan to operate; who actually builds one — the contracting models (design-bid-build, design-build, EPC turnkey) and which party carries which risk; and what it costs — capex versus opex, dollars per megawatt, and TCO as a discipline rather than a footnote.

    W Layer 3 Appendix

  2. 2 Design Requirements, Capacity & Load Planning pending

    The owner's side of the table. Owner's Project Requirements and Basis of Design; density-first capacity planning — deciding kW per rack, because that single number drives power, cooling, and structure downstream; availability frameworks (Uptime Tiers, TIA-942 ratings, BICSI classes) as one design criterion among several rather than the spine; codes; certifications and audits as facility concerns.

    W Layer 3 Appendix

  3. 3 Site Selection, Grid Interconnection, Water & Procurement pending

    Where it goes and whether you can actually get what you need. Location, climate, and natural-hazard factors; grid interconnection as a multi-year process, not a wiring job; water sourcing, discharge permitting, and community moratoriums; taxes, incentives, and permitting risk; and long-lead equipment procurement — why transformers and switchgear get ordered before the design is finished.

    R Layer 3 Appendix

  4. 4 Building & Physical Infrastructure pending

    Shell and core; structural loading, now driven by racks weighing over a tonne; raised floor versus slab; room topology (white space, gray space, MEP galleries); loading docks and equipment logistics; seismic, flood, and wind hardening; roof and envelope; noise as a permitting constraint.

    W Layer 3 Appendix

  5. 5 Power Systems I — Utility to UPS pending

    Utility feed and substations; transformers and switchgear; generators and fuel systems; UPS topologies (double-conversion, line-interactive, rotary) and the batteries behind them (VRLA, lithium-ion). Everything between the grid and the point where clean power becomes available to distribute.

    R Layer 3 Appendix

  6. 6 Power Systems II — Distribution & Redundancy pending

    Distribution from UPS to rack: PDUs, busway, remote power panels. Redundancy models (N, N+1, 2N, 2N+1, distributed redundant) and what each actually buys. The shift to 48V and higher-voltage DC for high-density racks. Grounding and bonding; emergency power off.

    R Layer 3 Appendix

  7. 7 Cooling & Thermal Management pending

    Heat and airflow fundamentals; CRAC versus CRAH; chilled-water plants; direct and indirect economization; hot/cold-aisle containment; liquid and immersion cooling as the mainstream path for high density, not an exotic appendix — cold plates, CDUs, rear-door heat exchangers; ASHRAE envelopes and humidity control; water use and heat reuse.

    R Layer 3 Appendix

  8. 8 AI/HPC Infrastructure + Compute & Storage pending

    AI clusters as a design driver: what megawatt racks change across power, cooling, network, floor loading, and cost. Then the general case — racks and rack units, server platforms, storage architectures (DAS, SAN, NAS, object, disaggregated), virtualization and orchestration at a glance, and where density is heading.

    R Layer 3 Appendix

  9. 9 Network Architecture & Structured Cabling pending

    The logical layer: spine-leaf (Clos) fabrics, ToR versus EoR, underlay and overlay (BGP, EVPN/VXLAN), AI fabrics (InfiniBand, 800G), peering and data-center interconnect, out-of-band management. The physical layer that carries it: copper and fiber media, TIA-942 spaces (MDA/IDA/HDA/EDA), overhead versus underfloor pathways, cross-connects, labeling, meet-me rooms and carrier entry.

    R Layer 3 Appendix

  10. 10 Physical Security, Fire Protection, Monitoring & Efficiency pending

    Layered security from perimeter to rack; access control, mantraps, surveillance; fire detection (spot, VESDA) and suppression (pre-action, clean agent); egress and life safety. Then the instrumentation layer: sensors and metering, BMS versus DCIM versus EPMS, alerting, capacity dashboards, asset and change tracking — and PUE/WUE/CUE, their limits, and efficiency levers, taught where they are actually measured.

    W Layer 3 Appendix

  11. 11 Commissioning, Operations & Decommissioning pending

    Proving it works, running it, and ending it. Commissioning levels 1–5; factory and site acceptance; the independent commissioning agent and why independence is the point; integrated systems testing (pull-the-plug day); documentation and handover. Then MOPs/SOPs/EOPs, preventive maintenance, staffing and the labor shortage, change and incident management, vendor and spares strategy. Finally decommissioning: ITAD, data sanitization, and chain of custody.

    R Layer 3 Appendix

  12. 12 Cross-Topic Threads (synthesis — built days 13–14) pending

    Follow a watt (utility → CPU); follow a bit (internet → server → back); follow a BTU (chip → outside air); the utility-failure timeline, second by second; follow a dollar (budget → procurement → installed capacity); and how one decision — rack density — ripples across power, cooling, building, network, and operations.

    — Layer 3 Appendix
