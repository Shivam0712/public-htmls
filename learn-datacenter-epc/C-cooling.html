Card 8 of 72mechanism

What are cold-air bypass and hot-air recirculation, and how can excessive or insufficient static pressure produce these faults?

Cold-air bypass occurs when supplied cold air short-circuits directly to the return instead of passing through servers. Excessive static pressure can force this wasteful flow. Hot-air recirculation occurs when server exhaust re-enters equipment intakes; insufficient pressure can starve those intakes of cold air. Set pressure only high enough to overcome the airflow path, and separate hot and cold streams with containment.

Card 10 of 72mechanism

Why does ASHRAE favor dew-point control over a fixed relative-humidity target, and what risks are addressed by its recommended dew-point range?

A fixed relative-humidity percentage represents different amounts of actual moisture at different air temperatures. Dew point tracks that moisture more directly, so it better guides humidity control around equipment. ASHRAE’s recommended dew-point band is roughly −9 °C to 15 °C: the lower end helps guard against electrostatic discharge when air is too dry; the upper end addresses moisture-related dust and corrosion risks.

How does hot- or cold-aisle containment suppress bypass and recirculation while allowing warmer supply air, lower fan demand, and fewer hot spots?

Containment physically separates cold supply air from hot exhaust. This limits bypass—cold air returning without cooling equipment—and recirculation, where hot exhaust re-enters server intakes. Less recirculation reduces hot spots; less mixing lets operators use warmer supply air and a larger temperature rise, reducing required airflow and fan energy. Reported benefits include roughly doubled *effective* cooling capacity, not doubled installed capacity.
